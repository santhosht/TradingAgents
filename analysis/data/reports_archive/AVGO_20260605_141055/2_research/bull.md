## Round 2 — Rebuttal & Expansion

The bear's case rests on three pillars: (1) software revenue miss invalidates the AI premium, (2) $64B in debt is a balance sheet liability, and (3) the macro/SpaceX rotation headwind makes near-term entry dangerous. Let me take each one apart.

**On the software miss:** The bear is confusing a single-quarter fluctuation with a structural trend reversal. Look at operating income: $5.9B → $6.1B → $7.7B → $8.7B. That's a staircase, not a stumble. The software revenue component that "missed" is still growing — it just grew slower than one quarter's consensus estimate. AVGO's AI software revenue is driven by custom ASIC deployment timelines set by hyperscalers — Google, Meta, ByteDance. These contracts don't fluctuate month-to-month based on macro conditions; they're multi-year infrastructure build-outs. One quarter's recognition timing does not invalidate the $100B trajectory.

Moreover, let's talk about what "software" means in AVGO's context. Post-VMware, approximately 40% of AVGO's revenue is infrastructure software (VMware, Symantec, CA Technologies). These are enterprise-sticky, high-renewal-rate products. The AI ASIC hardware/chip component is separately classified. A miss in the software segment could be as simple as recognition timing for VMware multi-year contracts — not a demand destruction signal.

**On the $64B debt:** $64B sounds terrifying until you compare it to $27.2B in annual FCF. That's a 2.35× debt/annual-FCF ratio. Apple runs at similar ratios. The working capital improvement from $1.6B (Q2 FY2025) to $15.2B (Q1 FY2026) shows the balance sheet is self-healing. Broadcom is not a distressed credit situation — it's a highly leveraged, highly profitable machine that generates enough cash to service its debt and buy back $7.85B in stock in a single quarter simultaneously.

**On the SpaceX rotation:** Yes, SpaceX IPO on June 12 will pull capital from liquid tech names. But this is a 5–10 day mechanical headwind, not a fundamental change. The correct response is patience, not panic. If AVGO drops to $397–$400 (50-day SMA) due to SpaceX rotation selling, that's where disciplined buyers step in — not where they step back.

**Adding to the bull case:** RSI at 43.84 is approaching but not yet at 30. Historical precedent for AVGO's prior post-earnings dips (NVDA has had multiple 10–20% selloffs that recovered fully within 30–60 days) supports mean reversion. Volume of 80.9M on June 4 (capitulation-level) is typically the "max pain" session — a flush that clears out weak hands and sets up institutional accumulation. The Q1 FY2026 stock buyback of $7.85B demonstrates management's own conviction in buying AVGO at $350–$420 — they're doing exactly what I'm recommending.
