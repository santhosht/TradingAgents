
## Round 1

# Bear Case — AMD — Round 1

---

The bull just handed you a sales pitch dressed up as analysis. Let me show you what the data actually says — and why AMD at $466 after a -10.86% single-day crash is not a "gift." It is a warning.

## Rebuttal: The Selloff Is NOT Just Macro

The bull claims today's decline is "100% macro." That is wishful framing. Let me point to what the data shows: AMD fell 10.86% vs. NVDA's 5.9% — nearly double the sector-average decline. The Nasdaq fell 4.2%. AMD's beta of 2.49 explains some of this (2.49 × 4.2% = ~10.5%), but the actual decline at 10.86% is still above even beta-adjusted expectations. More importantly, this selloff came on 46.7M shares — the highest volume of the entire 20-day window, matching the accumulation day of May 8. When a stock distributes on equal volume to its accumulation day, that is institutional selling, not macro noise.

## Rebuttal: The EPS Ramp Is Fantasy

Q1 2026 EPS: $0.84. The consensus forward EPS is $13.08. To reach $13.08 annually, AMD needs approximately $3.27/quarter average through Q4 2026. From $0.84. That is a 4× quarterly EPS increase that must happen in three quarters. Here is what the data actually shows: Gross profit DECLINED in Q1 2026 ($5.416B) vs. Q4 2025 ($5.577B) despite revenue being essentially flat. Operating income DECLINED: $1.476B vs. $1.752B. This is margin compression, not expansion. If AMD cannot maintain margins at $10.25B in quarterly revenue, how does it achieve a 4× EPS ramp?

## The Valuation Is Not Cheap — It Prices In Perfection

The bull says forward P/E of 35.67× is "dirt cheap." The PEG of 1.12 assumes analysts have correctly estimated AMD's growth rate. But those analyst estimates already require AMD to nearly quadruple its quarterly EPS in three quarters. If the EPS ramp hits 70% of expectations, AMD's forward P/E at $466 is not 35.67× — it is closer to 50×. At 50× forward P/E in a rising rate environment with a beaten-down Nasdaq, AMD's "cheap" valuation disappears. The analyst mean target of $482.69 is barely 3.5% above current price. This is not a stock with a wide margin of safety.

## Insider Selling Is a Screaming Red Flag

- Mark Papermaster (CTO): 3,293 shares (April 6), 6,000 shares (April 15), 33,109 shares (April 16), 31,320 shares (April 24), 2,350 shares (May 11) — over 76,000 shares total in 6 weeks

This is not "diversification" by one executive. This is systematic, coordinated selling by AMD's CEO, CTO, and multiple officers across March, April, and May — precisely as AMD was rallying from $114 to $546. These people know AMD's business better than any analyst.

AMD's China data center revenue is not disclosed separately, but any restriction hits AMD's highest-margin GPU segment.

For a company supposedly entering a hypergrowth profit ramp, a 15.7% quarter-over-quarter operating income decline in the most recent quarter is a serious execution concern.

To achieve $3.27/quarter EPS on 1.63B diluted shares requires approximately $5.3B quarterly net income. At 13.4% current net margin, AMD needs ~$40B quarterly revenue to generate that EPS. AMD's TTM revenue is $37.45B annually. This math is impossible without massive revenue acceleration AND margin expansion occurring simultaneously. The only way $13.08 forward EPS is achievable is if AMD's data center GPU segment creates extraordinary operating leverage at much higher margins. The Q1 2026 data shows the opposite — margins are compressing, not expanding.

## Rebuttal: Government AI Catalyst — Hope Is Not a Strategy

The AI executive meeting is mentioned in StockTwits only — not confirmed in news data. Even if it occurs, government AI spending announcements benefit Nvidia first. Japan's $500M "Genesis Mission" pledge does not translate to AMD revenue in Q2 or Q3 2026.
