# Fundamentals Analyst Report — AMD
**Run ID:** AMD_20260605_151644 | **Date:** 2026-06-05

---

## Overview

AMD presents a fundamentally compelling but richly valued picture. The company is executing an extraordinary earnings inflection — from $2.98 TTM EPS to $13.01 forward EPS — while maintaining a pristine balance sheet and accelerating free cash flow generation. The core question is whether the 336% forward EPS growth estimate is realistic and whether the current valuation ($487.50 live) adequately prices execution risk on that inflection.

---

## Valuation Analysis

### P/E (TTM) — 163.59×
AMD trades at 163.59× trailing twelve-month earnings of $2.98/share. This is an extremely elevated valuation by any historical standard for a semiconductor company. However, it is nearly irrelevant as a forward-looking metric given the magnitude of the EPS inflection underway.

**Why the TTM P/E is misleading here:**
- AMD's TTM includes Q2 2025 ($0.54 EPS) and Q3 2025 ($0.75 EPS) — quarters reflecting a business still ramping AI infrastructure revenue
- Q1 2026 delivered $0.84 EPS — the trajectory is clearly upward
- Forward EPS of $13.01 implies the market is pricing in a 4–5× earnings jump from current run rates

### Forward P/E — 37.47×
At $487.50 (live), AMD's forward P/E is 37.47×. This is elevated relative to the S&P 500 (~21×) and the semiconductor sector median (~25–30×), but it is the relevant metric for valuation assessment.

**Is 37.47× forward P/E justified?**
- The PEG ratio is 1.31 — suggesting reasonable-to-fair value if the growth rate materializes
- For context: NVDA trades at ~35–40× forward earnings despite its dominant AI position; AMD at 37× is valued similarly but with a less dominant market share
- The justification entirely depends on AMD achieving ~$13 EPS. If forward EPS is revised down, the multiple expansion required would be unsustainable at current prices.

### PEG Ratio — 1.31
PEG of 1.31 is in the "reasonable" range (1.0 = perfectly priced for growth, below 1.0 = cheap, above 2.0 = expensive). At 1.31, AMD is modestly above fair-value-for-growth, not egregiously overvalued relative to its growth trajectory. This is one of the most constructive valuation metrics in the dataset.

### Price/Book — 12.33×
At $39.55 book value and $487.50 price, AMD trades at 12.33× book. Tangible book value is only ~$14/share (equity of $64.5B minus goodwill/intangibles of $41.5B = $23B tangible equity / 1.63B shares ≈ $14). On tangible book, AMD trades at ~35×. This premium is driven by the embedded value of AMD's IP (CPU/GPU architecture), customer relationships, and the Xilinx acquisition, all of which have real value but are not on the balance sheet as tangible assets.

### EV/EBITDA — 113.68×
At $7.43B EBITDA on a TTM basis, EV/EBITDA of 113.68× is extremely stretched. This is a trailing metric and, like TTM P/E, reflects a business in mid-ramp. On a forward basis (if EBITDA scales proportionally with the EPS inflection), EV/EBITDA would compress dramatically. However, this metric reinforces that AMD is priced to perfection on trailing fundamentals.

## Profitability Analysis

### Revenue Trajectory
| Quarter | Revenue | QoQ Growth |
|---------|---------|-----------|
| Q2 2025 | $7.685B | — |
| Q3 2025 | $9.246B | +20.3% |
| Q4 2025 | $10.270B | +11.1% |
| Q1 2026 | $10.253B | -0.2% |
| **TTM** | **$37.45B** | — |

Revenue growth has been explosive: from $7.7B in Q2 2025 to $10.3B in Q1 2026 — a 33.4% increase in three quarters. The Q1 2026 slight sequential decline ($10.27B → $10.25B) is notable and bears watching. Is this seasonality or a plateau? Management guidance for Q2 2026 would be the key data point (not in this dataset).

### Gross Margin — 53.1% (TTM), $5.42B in Q1 2026
AMD's gross margin of 53.1% on $37.45B revenue is strong and improving:
- Q2 2025: $3.06B gross profit on $7.69B = 39.8% margin — very low
- Q3 2025: $4.78B / $9.25B = 51.7%
- Q4 2025: $5.58B / $10.27B = 54.3%
- Q1 2026: $5.42B / $10.25B = 52.9%

The gross margin has expanded dramatically from 39.8% to ~53% in four quarters, driven by a higher-margin product mix (MI300X AI accelerators have higher ASPs than traditional CPUs). Slight sequential dip from Q4 to Q1 warrants monitoring but is within normal quarterly variance.

### Operating Margin — 14.4% (TTM)
Operating margin is lower than gross margin due to AMD's significant R&D investment:
- Q1 2026: $1.476B operating income on $10.253B = 14.4%
- R&D alone: $2.397B in Q1 2026 (23.4% of revenue)
- SG&A: $1.253B in Q1 2026 (12.2% of revenue)

AMD is investing heavily in R&D — over $9B annualized — to stay competitive with NVIDIA in AI accelerators and Intel in CPUs. This is necessary investment but it compresses operating margins. As revenue scales with AI demand, operating leverage should expand margins significantly.

### Profit Margin — 13.4% (TTM), $1.383B Q1 2026
Net income is growing: Q2 2025 $872M → Q3 2025 $1.243B → Q4 2025 $1.511B → Q1 2026 $1.383B. The Q4→Q1 dip in net income (despite stable revenue) reflects higher tax/interest expenses in Q1 — common seasonal pattern.

### ROE — 8.1% | ROA — 3.6%
These are relatively modest returns on equity and assets, reflecting AMD's current investment phase. With equity of $64.5B (largely from the Xilinx acquisition), ROE will remain suppressed until earnings fully ramp. Forward ROE, if $13 EPS materializes, would approach 30%+ — a dramatic improvement.

## Balance Sheet Analysis

### Cash Position — $12.35B (Q1 2026)
AMD's cash and short-term investments position grew from $5.87B (Q2 2025) to $12.35B (Q1 2026) — more than doubling in three quarters. This is exceptional cash generation. At the current rate of FCF generation ($2.5B+ per quarter), AMD's net cash position will continue expanding rapidly.

### Debt — $3.87B | Net Cash — ~$8.5B
Total debt of $3.87B against $12.35B cash = net cash position of approximately $8.48B. AMD is a net cash company with no near-term refinancing risk. D/E ratio of 6.0 appears concerning but is driven by the denominator (equity of $64.5B) being largely composed of intangibles — on a debt-to-tangible-equity basis, leverage is higher but still manageable.

### Current Ratio — 2.73
Current ratio of 2.73 indicates AMD has $2.73 of current assets for every $1 of current liabilities. Working capital of $18.1B is robust. No near-term liquidity concerns.

### Inventory — $8.05B (Q1 2026)
Inventory has grown from $6.68B (Q2 2025) to $8.05B (Q1 2026) — a $1.37B build over three quarters. For context, against quarterly revenue of $10.25B, AMD is carrying ~28 days of inventory. This is a modest increase but worth monitoring: inventory builds can precede a demand slowdown or reflect production ramp-up for anticipated demand (MI400 series).

### Goodwill and Intangibles — $41.5B
AMD carried the Xilinx acquisition at $35B+ in goodwill/intangibles. This creates a potential impairment risk if Xilinx-related business deteriorates, though there are no current signs of this. Tangible book of $22.96B is the "real" equity floor.

FCF of $2.566B in Q1 2026 alone represents a $10.3B annualized FCF run rate. At market cap of $794.92B (live), AMD trades at ~77× annualized Q1 FCF. On TTM FCF of $7.17B, it's 111× FCF. These are premium multiples.

### Capex — $389M in Q1 2026
AMD is a fabless semiconductor company — it outsources manufacturing to TSMC. Capex is therefore minimal (primarily for design equipment, data infrastructure). Capex-to-revenue ratio of ~3.8% in Q1 is very low compared to integrated semiconductor manufacturers. This is a structural FCF advantage: AMD captures software-like margins on hardware without the massive capital intensity of fabs.

### Share Buybacks — $355M in Q1 2026
AMD bought back $355M of stock in Q1 2026 at average prices in the $440–$500 range (based on quarterly price range). This is modest relative to FCF ($2.566B) and market cap ($794B). AMD is directing most FCF to cash accumulation rather than buybacks — prudent given the ongoing competitive investment cycle.

## EPS Inflection Analysis

### TTM EPS ($2.98) → Forward EPS ($13.01) — 336% Growth Implied

This is the defining fundamental question for AMD. The forward EPS of $13.01 implies analysts expect AMD to roughly 4.4× its earnings in the next 12 months. Breaking this down:

- Q1 2026 run rate: $0.84 EPS × 4 = $3.36 annualized — FAR below $13.01 forward estimate
- To achieve $13.01 forward EPS, AMD would need approximately $3.25/share per quarter for each of the next 4 quarters — nearly 4× current quarterly run rate

**What justifies the $13.01 forward EPS estimate?**
The forward EPS estimate is almost certainly pricing in a step-function inflection driven by:
1. **MI300X to MI400 transition** — AMD's next-generation AI accelerator, if widely adopted, could dramatically increase revenue and margin per chip
2. **Continued EPYC CPU market share gains** — AMD is displacing Intel in data center CPUs; continued share gains at higher ASPs drive revenue
3. **Data center mix shift** — AI accelerators carry 60–70% gross margins vs. traditional CPUs at 50%+; mix shift improves blended margins
4. **Revenue scale leverage** — R&D and SG&A relatively fixed; revenue doubling would dramatically expand operating margins

**Risk:** $13.01 forward EPS requires near-perfect execution. Any guidance miss — especially on AI accelerator revenue — would cause significant multiple compression.

## Fundamental Assessment

AMD is a **fundamentally excellent but fully-valued company** at current prices. The business is executing well: revenue is scaling, margins are expanding, cash is accumulating, and the AI/CPU opportunity is large and real. However, at 37.47× forward P/E with that forward EPS requiring a 336% earnings inflection, the valuation leaves no margin for execution error.

The key risk is not "is AMD a good company?" — it clearly is. The risk is "is AMD executing fast enough to justify 37× forward earnings while trading above analyst consensus at $487?"

**Fundamental verdict:** Attractive long-term entry below $450; current prices ($487–$523) are fair-to-slightly-stretched assuming forward EPS of $13.01 is achievable. The next quarterly earnings report is the critical catalyst — a beat raises targets, a miss causes significant derating.
