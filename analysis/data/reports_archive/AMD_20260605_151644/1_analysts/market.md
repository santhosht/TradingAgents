## Trend Analysis

### Primary Trend — Violently Bullish, Now Pausing

AMD's last 20 sessions tell a remarkable story: the stock bottomed near $393 intraday on May 19 and surged 39% to a 52-week high of $546.44 on June 3 — in 11 trading days. That move has now stalled. The last completed candle (June 4) closed at $523.20, down from the prior day's $542.52 high. Today's live quote ($487.50, -5.18%) shows the selloff accelerating, with the stock now $59 below the 52W high.

Despite this two-day retreat of ~$55 ($542.52 → $487.50 live), the primary trend remains emphatically bullish on all three moving average timeframes:

- **EMA 10 ($503.63):** Price was 3.9% above this on June 4. Today's live price ($487.50) has crossed below the EMA 10 for the first time since the rally began. This is the first meaningful short-term caution signal — a close below EMA 10 would be a notable break after 11+ sessions of price riding above it.

- **SMA 50 ($353.80):** Price is 47.9% above the 50-day average. This extreme extension — nearly $170 above — reflects the explosive pace of this rally. The SMA 50 is a very distant support floor; it is not an actionable near-term level.

- **SMA 200 ($244.47):** Price is 114% above the 200-day average. This is a historically extreme reading. It reflects AMD's transformation from a $114 (52W low) stock to near $550 in under 12 months. The 200 SMA is long-term support and confirms the secular bull market in AMD is intact.

### VWMA 20 ($467.94)

The volume-weighted moving average sits at $467.94. This is a critical near-term level because it represents the average price weighted by actual traded volume over the past 20 days. Price ($487.50 live) is holding above VWMA 20 for now, but a close below $467 would signal that sellers have overcome the volume-weighted cost basis of the recent buyers — a bearish near-term signal.

### RSI (14) — 67.01

RSI at 67.01 is elevated and approaching overbought territory (70 threshold) but has not crossed it on the last completed candle. This partial mean-reversion in RSI is constructive: if the stock can consolidate and RSI cycles back down toward 50–55, it creates a healthier base for the next leg.

Money Flow Index (volume-weighted RSI) at 65.51 parallels the RSI reading — elevated but not extreme. This confirms that the recent buying was accompanied by real volume. The MFI not hitting 70+ despite the 39% rally suggests the buying pressure, while aggressive, was not speculative blow-off in nature.

## Volatility Analysis

### ATR (14) — $29.38

AMD's 14-day ATR is $29.38, meaning the average true range over the past two weeks is roughly $29 per day. Today's intraday range alone (high $505.62, low $484.26) is $21.36 at time of capture — consistent with elevated daily volatility. Key implications:

1. **Stop loss placement:** Any stop should be placed minimum 1.5×ATR = $44.07 below entry to avoid noise washouts. A stop at $487.50 – $44.07 = $443.43 would represent the minimum protective floor.
2. **Normal daily noise range:** Moves of $15–$35 in a day are expected and do not constitute trend reversals by themselves.
3. **Beta context:** AMD's Beta of 2.49 means it moves ~2.5× the S&P 500. On a -1% market day, AMD will typically drop ~2.5%. Today's selloff is consistent with a sector-wide semiconductor decline, not an AMD-specific breakdown.
