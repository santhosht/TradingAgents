## Balanced Assessment — Challenging Both Extremes

Both my colleagues made valid points and both overstated their cases. Let me synthesize.

**To the Aggressive Analyst:** You are right that the risk-reward is compelling and that AMD's dual CPU/GPU franchise is a multi-year opportunity. But 6% in a Beta 2.49 name is position sizing for certainty, not risk-adjusted opportunity. And your $700 stretch target is not grounded in any valuation model in this dataset. You're letting enthusiasm drive numbers.

**To the Conservative Analyst:** You are right that inventory build, insider selling, and forward EPS optimism are genuine risks. But 3% allocation in a Overweight-rated setup with $665 analyst high target and 3.4:1 R:R to Target 2 is undersizing a high-conviction opportunity. Your $520 Target 1 cuts the position before AMD even retests its 52W high — that's not conservative risk management, that's leaving the majority of the thesis unrealized.

**My balanced view on position size:**

4% is correct. The trader's original proposal is grounded. Here's the logic:
- At 4% allocation and $44 stop risk, maximum loss = 0.44% of total portfolio — well within a 2% single-position risk budget
- Beta 2.49 with a hard $428 stop and 4% allocation is manageable
- 6% (aggressive) crosses the line: same Beta creates oversized portfolio exposure
- 3% (conservative) is excessively punitive for what is rated Overweight with strong fundamental support

**On the forward EPS debate:**

The truth is between the aggressive ($12–15 achievable on MI400) and the bear/conservative ($4–7 realistic). My assessment: $8–10 EPS within 18 months is achievable with strong-but-not-perfect execution. That assumes:
- Data Center GPU revenue grows 40–50% annually (not tripling in 12 months)
- EPYC CPU continues gaining 2–3 share points per quarter vs Intel
- Operating leverage kicks in above $12B quarterly revenue

At $9 EPS and 37× forward P/E → $333. At $9 EPS and 45× premium multiple → $405. This is below current prices. What closes the gap? The market is not just pricing $9 EPS — it's pricing the optionality of the MI400 cycle delivering $12–15 EPS. That optionality has value, but it should be sized for, not bet on at 6%.

**On the inventory build:**

The conservative analyst is right to flag it. I reviewed the data: $1.37B inventory build over 3 quarters. But context matters: AMD is building inventory ahead of the MI400 launch cycle — standard practice for a product launch ramp. The inventory build is not inherently demand-softening if it coincides with production ramp for a new product launch. We'd need to know the MI400 launch timeline (not in this dataset) to fully assess this risk. I give this a yellow flag, not a red flag. Monitor in Q2.

**On insider selling:**

Both the conservative and aggressive analyst's positions are partially right. Lisa Su selling $72M across two tranches in two months is not alarming in isolation — it is large in dollar terms but represents <0.1% of AMD's float. Institutional accumulation ongoing. However: the absence of ANY insider purchase across the entire executive team is notable. In high-conviction periods, you see at least one insider buying. Zero purchases across CEO, CTO, CFO-equivalent over the entire dataset is worth noting. I weight this as a mild negative, not a strong signal.

This staged approach achieves a blended entry around $468–$472, keeps average cost in the highest-probability support zone, and limits commitment before seeing how the selloff resolves.
