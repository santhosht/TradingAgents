## Round 2 — Rebuttal of Bear R1

The bear made three main points: heavy insider selling, valuation stretched with execution risk, and sector derating. Let me take each one directly.

**On insider selling:** Lisa Su selling $55.7M of AMD shares is a 10b5-1 planned transaction — these are set up months in advance and have nothing to do with the CEO's real-time view of the stock. CEOs of high-growth companies are required to diversify their personal wealth; this is legal compliance, not a signal. If we flagged every CEO diversification sale as a red flag, we'd never buy any company run by a well-compensated executive. More importantly: the bear pointed to insider selling but ignored that AMD's institutional ownership has been *increasing* — major funds are accumulating shares that insiders are diversifying. Insiders selling for personal financial planning reasons while institutions accumulate is a bullish divergence, not bearish.

**On execution risk for $13.01 forward EPS:** The bear assumes AMD needs to be at $3.25/quarter consistently to hit $13. That math ignores operating leverage. AMD's R&D at $2.4B/quarter is ~23.4% of current revenue. If revenue scales to $14B/quarter (driven by MI400 ramp + EPYC gains), R&D stays roughly flat — meaning it drops to ~17% of revenue. That 600bps margin improvement on $14B revenue is $840M per quarter in additional operating income. The path to $13 EPS is not linear — it's operating-leverage-driven, which is faster and more reliable than linear revenue growth. The bear is modeling AMD like a slow-growth company when it should be modeled like a company hitting an inflection point in its cost-revenue dynamics.

**On sector derating and AVGO:** The bear says AVGO's guidance validates a slowdown in AI. But AVGO's AI exposure is concentrated in custom AI chips (ASIC co-development with hyperscalers) — not standard GPUs or CPUs that AMD sells. When AVGO guides soft, it means hyperscalers are spending less on AVGO's *custom silicon*. That could actually be directionally positive for AMD if hyperscalers are redirecting spend toward AMD's standard accelerators (MI300X/MI400), which are more flexible and software-compatible than custom ASICs. The bear conflated "AVGO AI soft" with "all AI soft" — those are different markets.

**The PEG of 1.31 doesn't lie.** For a company with AMD's growth trajectory, a PEG of 1.31 is a reasonable entry point. The bears want to use the TTM P/E of 163× as their valuation anchor, but that's like valuing Netflix at its 2005 P/E — you'd have missed the entire bull run. Forward-looking multiples for inflection-point companies are the relevant metric.

## Round 3 — Final Rebuttal of Bear R2

The bear doubled down on two things in Round 2: the Q1 revenue flatness (implying plateau) and the macro rate risk. Let me close out both arguments.

**On the Q1 revenue plateau argument:**

The bear pointed to Q4 2025 ($10.27B) vs Q1 2026 ($10.25B) as evidence of revenue peaking. This is selective data reading. Quarter-over-quarter flatness in Q1 is *expected seasonality* in semiconductors — Q4 is always the strongest quarter for hardware (enterprise year-end buying, consumer holiday). The sequential comparison that matters is Q1 2026 vs Q1 2025. AMD didn't break out Q1 2025 in this dataset, but the trend from Q2 2025 ($7.69B) to Q1 2026 ($10.25B) over three quarters is unambiguously positive. Calling Q4→Q1 flatness a plateau is cherry-picking the weakest sequential compare in the calendar year.

More importantly: if AMD Q2 2026 guidance (which we don't have in this dataset) shows sequential growth — which every sell-side model projects — the "plateau" narrative dissolves immediately. AMD's management has an outstanding record of beating and raising guidance. Until we see an actual revenue miss or guide-down, Q1 flatness is noise.

**On macro rate risk:**

Yes, rates are higher for longer. Yes, AMD at 37× forward P/E is sensitive to this. But consider: the Fed's path to rate cuts has not changed — the market still expects 1–2 cuts by year-end. More importantly, AMD's customers — hyperscalers and enterprise data centers — are spending on AI infrastructure not because rates are low but because AI capability creates direct competitive advantage. Microsoft, Google, and Amazon are not going to pause their AI data center buildout because the Fed held rates. These are strategic, multi-year investments. The interest rate sensitivity for AMD's *demand* is far lower than the rate sensitivity for AMD's *multiple*.

What the bull case ultimately comes down to is this: AMD is one of two companies on earth that can supply AI accelerators at scale to global data centers. The other is NVIDIA. If you believe AI infrastructure buildout continues — and the hyperscalers' own capex guidance says it does — then AMD is a buy at any price that represents a discount to its forward earnings power. At $487.50, at 37× forward P/E with a PEG of 1.31, AMD is not cheap but it is not egregiously expensive for what it is. The risk-reward on a 12-month basis is firmly positive.
