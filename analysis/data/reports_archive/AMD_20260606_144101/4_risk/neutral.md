A well-defined stop and a high-Beta position require **inverse sizing, not proportionate sizing**. 12% position with 11% stop-loss = 1.32% portfolio risk — at the edge of responsible single-trade risk. In a portfolio with other positions, 12% in a Beta-2.49 stock creates correlated drawdown risk the aggressive analyst's single-stock math doesn't capture.

The insider selling dismissal is too casual. $73M CEO sales + $18.4M CTO sales + multiple officer sales + zero purchases in 3 months is not "routine diversification" — it deserves more analytical weight.

The $665 high analyst target is cherry-picking. The correct planning assumption is the mean ($482.69), not the optimistic tail.

Against a $51 stop (11%), that's 1.05:1 to first target — not the 0.32:1 distortion the conservative analyst presented.

Waiting for Q2'26 costs real money if AMD recovers from $466 to $527 before earnings. Capital protection means not missing recoveries in fundamentally sound companies.
