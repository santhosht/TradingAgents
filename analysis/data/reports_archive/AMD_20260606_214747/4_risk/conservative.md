## Attacking "Gift-Wrapped Entry"

$466 is not a gift — it is a parabolic-run price at a 14.7% discount from an extreme. AMD trades at 156× TTM earnings and 101× EV/EBITDA. These are not gift prices. The correct anchor is not the all-time high — it is forward fair value, which at $13.08 Forward EPS and a reasonable 30–35× forward P/E multiple = $392–$458. The stock is near the *top* of fair value, not a discount.

## Stop at $380 Is Reckless

$467 entry, $380 stop = $87/share drawdown = 18.6% per-share loss. For a 10% portfolio position: 1.86% total portfolio loss from one stock. Combined with Beta 2.49 and a 10% position: one AMD bad day can dominate total portfolio performance. This is not "room to breathe" — this is accepting outsized portfolio-level risk.

## 8–10% Position Is a Concentration Trap

AMD Beta 2.49 × 10% allocation = ~25% market-beta equivalent exposure in one name. A semiconductor sector correction (China restrictions, data center overbuild, Nasdaq rate pressure) could have AMD accounting for 20–25% of total portfolio drawdown. Sizing to Beta protects capital.

7.6M job openings (near 2-year high), Iran war inflation (Eurozone 3.2%), elevated rates. The rate environment is NOT turning quickly. Buying AMD at 35.67× forward P/E in a sustained elevated-rate environment fights the discount rate on every dollar of future earnings.
