AMD dropped -10.86% on June 5. What triggered it? A strong jobs report and AVGO earnings overhang. Neither had anything to do with AMD's business. The jobs report is a macro event affecting all high-multiple growth stocks. AVGO's results are about Broadcom's custom silicon strategy — a different market segment. AMD's MI300 data center GPU ramp is intact. Not a single AMD-specific negative emerged this week. When the best AI semiconductor company (outside Nvidia) drops 11% for macro reasons, that is a gift.

Revenue: $7.685B (Q2'25) → $10.253B (Q1'26) = +33.4% growth. Gross margin: 39.8% → 52.8% = 1,300bps expansion. Operating CF: $2.011B → $2.955B = +47%. Cash: $5.87B → $12.35B — more than doubled. This is a company mid-ramp, not in decline.

TTM P/E of 156× is the wrong metric. Forward P/E of 35.67× on $13.08 EPS is correct. Even at 30% haircut to consensus ($9.15 EPS), AMD trades at ~51× forward — reasonable for peak AI infrastructure buildout. PEG of 1.12 confirms fair value relative to growth rate.

## TD Cowen Raised to $600 Post-Management Meeting

Post-management meeting raises signal improved business visibility. TD Cowen sat with management on June 1 and walked away more bullish, raising from $500 to $600. This is forward-looking color that earnings calls cannot provide.

## Fortress Balance Sheet

$12.35B cash, $3.87B debt = $8.48B net cash. Operating CF $2.955B/quarter ($11.8B annualized). FCF $7.17B TTM. AMD can fund R&D, buy back stock, and weather macro storms without raising capital.

## AI Secular Trend — Multi-Trillion Dollar Cycle

Japan AI Manhattan Project ($500M), U.S. Genesis Mission, hyperscaler capex expansion — this is a multi-year, multi-trillion-dollar buildout. AMD is one of only two companies that can supply enterprise-grade GPU accelerators at scale. Being #2 in this market is worth hundreds of billions in revenue over 5 years.

Lisa Su sold 125,000 shares worth ~$55.7M on May 13 at ~$445. She still holds an enormous position. Selling a small fraction of a massive gain after a stock goes from $100 to $500 is not a fundamental concern signal — it is routine.

## Round 2

# Bull Case — AMD — Round 2

---

## Rebuttal: "Distribution Signal"

High volume cuts both ways. May 8 saw 58.1M shares — a massive up day. June 5 down day was sector-wide: Nvidia -5.9%, Nasdaq -4.2%, entire chip complex sold off. AMD participating in a sector-wide flush is not specific institutional distribution. The bear cherry-picks a single data point from a broad market event.

## Rebuttal: "Fantasy EPS"

The Q1'26 vs Q4'25 EPS dip ($0.84 vs $0.92) reflects intentional R&D investment (+$67M to $2.40B). Revenue was essentially flat ($10.27B → $10.25B) in a seasonally soft Q1. The Data Center GPU ramp (MI300 and successors) accelerates in Q2–Q4. Forward EPS of $13.08 reflects analyst consensus from people who just met with AMD management. The bear shows trailing EPS doesn't reflect the ramp — that's the entire point of forward estimates.

## Rebuttal: "Nvidia Checkmate"

AMD doesn't need to beat Nvidia. It needs a portion of a massive expanding market. AMD Data Center segment alone: $3.7B+ in Q1'26 revenue, up dramatically YoY. Being a strong #2 in a $100B+ market is exceptional. China restrictions hurt Nvidia proportionally too.

## Rebuttal: "Wrong Direction Margins"

The bear takes the worst QoQ comparison. The YoY reality: Q2'25: -1.7% → Q1'26: 14.4%. AMD went from negative operating margins to 14.4% in four quarters. That is extraordinary operating leverage. The Q1 vs Q4 comparison is seasonality, not trend.

## Rebuttal: "Data Center Cancellations"

"50% cancelled" is a circulating rumor, not confirmed data. The actual data: AMD revenue grew from $7.7B to $10.3B/quarter in four quarters. TD Cowen raised to $600 after a management meeting. If the buildout were collapsing, revenue wouldn't be growing. The bear has a social media narrative; the bull has $10.3B/quarter in revenue.

## Rebuttal: "Inventory Warning"

$8.045B inventory on $10.25B revenue = 78% of quarterly revenue. Normal for a hypergrowth semiconductor building stock to meet demand. 2022–2023 was a COVID demand collapse — there is no comparable structural demand driver today for that scenario.

## Rebuttal: "Systematic Insider Selling"

Lisa Su sold 210,000 shares = less than 3% of her total estimated position. All sales are ordinary RSU vesting-related. Every tech executive with RSU compensation sells shares at vest. The bear presents routine compensation-related selling as a warning — it isn't.

## Rebuttal: "Stop Level"

$447–$449 consolidation zone is real support. SMA 50 at $358 and SMA 200 at $246 are massive institutional support layers below. The stop at $415 provides appropriate buffer through known support levels.

## Core Case Unchanged

Revenue +33% YoY. Gross margin 52.8%. $8.48B net cash. FCF $7.17B TTM. PEG 1.12. Analysts raising after management meetings. 14.7% off 52-week high. The AI secular trend is the largest technology wave in decades. AMD is a BUY.

# Bull Case — AMD — Round 3

---

## Rebuttal: "Accumulation vs Distribution" Post-Hoc Narrative

May 8's surge was buying into strength from a macro-driven pullback — identical context to today. The bear's volume thesis requires the same event type to mean different things by direction. It doesn't. Both moves were macro-correlated.

## Rebuttal: The Forward EPS Math the Bear Won't Do

Q1'26: $5.416B gross profit on $10.25B revenue. To reach $3.27/quarter EPS at $14–15B revenue (33% growth for 4 quarters): 52% GM on $15B = $7.8B gross profit. OpEx scaling proportionally ≈ $4.5B. Operating income ~$3.3B, net income ~$2.8B+. That is 2–3× current NI, not 4.4×. The bear refuses to model the forward revenue state. The forward EPS is stretch, not fantasy.

## Rebuttal: "Nvidia Share-Taking" Thesis Wrong

AMD Data Center went from sub-$1B quarters in 2023 to $3.7B+ in Q1'26 — not by taking share from Nvidia but by participating in a market that expanded from near-zero to enormous. AMD doesn't need any Nvidia share to grow 30% annually with the market. The valuation prices market participation, not Nvidia-killing.

## Rebuttal: China Proportionality

AMD's China restrictions are *less severe* than Nvidia's because AMD is #2 in high-end AI. China restrictions hurt Nvidia proportionally more. AMD has been diversifying toward U.S. and European hyperscalers actively ramping MI300. The China risk is real but mispriced by the bear.

## Rebuttal: Margin "Trend" Is One Quarter Cherry-Picked

Q4'25: 17.1% was the outlier high, not the baseline. The YoY trend (from -1.7% to 14.4%) is the real direction. AMD guided for continued margin improvement through 2026. One R&D-investment quarter does not make a declining trend.

## Rebuttal: AVGO Cancellations Not Supported by Data

AVGO reported exceptional AI revenue growth in their custom silicon segment. The selloff was a market overreaction to sector sentiment, not to AVGO's actual numbers. AMD's own $10.3B quarterly revenue is the real evidence of demand — not social media rumors.

## Rebuttal: Inventory ≠ 2021 Micron

Micron 2021 was COVID demand collapse. AMD 2026 inventory supports hyperscaler AI commitments visible in Microsoft, Google, Meta, Amazon public capex disclosures. Conflating a COVID demand crash with hyperscaler structural AI buildout is a false analogy.

## Rebuttal: Insider Selling Is Portfolio Management

Lisa Su sold 4–7% of her estimated position at all-time highs after a 5× price increase. Every rational fiduciary does this for concentration management. Bears who cite this will be permanently bearish on every large-cap tech executive forever.
