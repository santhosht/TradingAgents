
## Round 1

# Bear Case — AMD — Round 1

---

## The Selloff Wasn't "Noise" — It's the Market Re-Pricing Risk

AMD dropped -10.86% on 46.7M shares — the highest volume in the 20-day sample outside May 8. That is institutions liquidating, not noise. High-volume capitulation after a 40% parabolic run is a distribution signal, not an opportunity. The bull wants you to buy the dip on a stock flashing major distribution.

## The 4.4× Forward EPS Ramp Is a Fantasy

The entire bull valuation case rests on $13.08 Forward EPS. Q1'26 EPS was $0.84 — to hit $13.08 annualized requires $3.27/quarter. The quarterly EPS progression is: $0.54 → $0.75 → $0.92 → $0.84. Q1'26 EPS is *lower* than Q4'25. AMD's earnings declined QoQ while revenue was flat ($10.27B → $10.25B). The ramp is decelerating, not accelerating. A 4.4× jump is not visible in any recent data.

## Nvidia Is Expanding Into New Markets While AMD Chases

The "Nvidia checkmate" narrative is real. Nvidia is capturing new multibillion-dollar markets. Senate hearings on China chip sales directly threaten AMD revenue — China is a significant portion of AMD's addressable market. More export restrictions are coming. The bull dismisses this; the market doesn't.

## Operating Margin Going the Wrong Direction

Q4'25 op margin: 17.1%. Q1'26 op margin: 14.4%. Sequential compression of 270bps. R&D is now $2.4B/quarter (23.4% of revenue). If R&D must grow to stay competitive with Nvidia, operating margins stay structurally capped. The path to $13.08 EPS requires margins going up — they are going down.

## AI Data Center Cancellations Are Not Noise

"50% AI data centers quietly cancelled" is circulating in markets and tied directly to why AVGO results hit the whole sector. If hyperscalers are pulling back on data center capex, AMD's MI300 GPU demand evaporates. The bull assumes the AI buildout is multi-year and already priced in — reality may be already disappointing consensus.

## Inventory Build Is a Classic Cycle Warning

Inventory: $6.677B (Q2'25) → $8.045B (Q1'26) = +20.5% in four quarters. In semiconductors, inventory builds precede demand destruction. When the cycle turns — and it always does — AMD faces painful inventory digestion, just like it did in 2022–2023. "Building to meet demand" is what every semi company says before the cycle peak.

## Parabolic Price Action Is Unsustainable

+38.9% in 11 trading days (May 19 to June 3). AMD is 89.6% above its 200-day SMA. Stocks this extended historically revert — hard. The risk/reward at current levels is asymmetric to the downside. The easy money has already been made.

## Insider Selling Is Systematic, Not Routine

Since March, insiders sold over 345,000 shares:
- Lisa Su: 210,000 shares (~$72.6M total) in March and May
- Papermaster (CTO): ~76,000 shares
- Grasby, Norrod, others: ~60,000+ shares

Clustered in the $430–$445 range — exactly where you're being asked to buy. This is systematic heavy selling at the top, not routine RSU disposal.

## The Stop Is Closer Than You Think

A stop at $415 looks safe — but below $466 the real support is $447–$449, then $420–$424. If AMD breaks $447, it very likely tests $420, which triggers your $415 stop. The parabolic run left almost no consolidation on the way up — minimal support on the way down.

**AMD is a SELL at these levels. Good businesses are terrible investments at the wrong price.**

## Round 2

# Bear Case — AMD — Round 2

---

## Rebuttal: "Volume Context"

May 8's 58.1M volume was accumulation into a depressed $418 price. June 5's 46.7M volume was distribution after a +39% run at a 52-week high. Accumulation at lows vs. distribution at highs are fundamentally different events. The bull conflates them deliberately.

## Rebuttal: "Intentional R&D Investment"

To reach $13.08 Forward EPS ($3.27/quarter), AMD needs to 4× earnings from $0.84 while R&D is growing $67M/quarter. Either R&D stops growing (losing the innovation race to Nvidia) or expenses grow with revenue, preventing the margin expansion required. The forward EPS requires simultaneous revenue acceleration AND margin expansion — neither is happening today.

## Rebuttal: "AMD Doesn't Need to Beat Nvidia"

AMD's entire AI GPU thesis IS predicated on taking share from Nvidia. But every data point this week shows Nvidia widening the moat: new market opportunities announced, Senate hearings focused on Nvidia dominance, analysts framing AMD as "after Nvidia." A 156× TTM P/E prices AMD as a Nvidia share-gainer, not a distant second-place. The market will reconcile this contradiction.

## Rebuttal: "YoY Operating Margin Improvement"

The YoY improvement (from -1.7% base-effect quarters) is real but misleading. The current trajectory that matters: Q3'25: 13.7% → Q4'25: 17.1% → Q1'26: 14.4% — peaked and declined. With R&D at $2.4B/quarter, the path to $3.27/quarter EPS is narrow even with revenue growth.

## Rebuttal: "Data Center Cancellations Are a Rumor"

AVGO earnings (which triggered the sector selloff) came from Broadcom, whose custom silicon directly competes for data center budgets with AMD's merchant silicon. The *entire chip sector* sold off on AVGO results — that is the market pricing AI data center narrative under pressure: custom silicon winning, cancellations circulating, overbuild concerns. Past revenue growing ≠ future revenue growing at 33%.

## Rebuttal: "Normal Inventory for Hypergrowth"

"Normal for hypergrowth" is what Micron, Nvidia, and AMD said in 2021 before the devastating 2022–2023 inventory correction. $8.045B on $10.25B revenue = 78% of quarterly revenue, up 20.5% in four quarters. If MI300 orders slow from cancellations, custom silicon competition, or export restrictions, $8B in inventory becomes a serious problem. Dismissing inventory build in semiconductors has repeatedly been the wrong call.

## Rebuttal: "Routine Insider Selling"

Lisa Su sold 85,000 shares in March at ~$199, then accelerated to 125,000 shares in May at ~$445. Papermaster made 5 separate ladder sales April 6–May 20. Every senior executive was a net seller into all-time highs. People who know the business best are selling into this parabolic run. If they saw $665 coming, they would be buying.

## Core Bear Position

AMD's fundamentals are good — no one disputes revenue growth. The question is price. At 156× TTM P/E and 4.4× forward EPS ramp required, the margin for error is zero. The bull requires: AI demand accelerating, Nvidia not dominating further, China exports not tightening, margins expanding while R&D grows, inventory clearing. That is a perfect-world scenario — and you are being asked to pay for it at $466 after a parabolic distribution day.

**AMD is a SELL or HOLD AT BEST.**

## Round 3

# Bear Case — AMD — Round 3

---

## Rebuttal: Volume Context

May 8 was recovery buying off multi-month lows after a tariff scare. June 5 was distribution at a 52-week high after a +39% parabolic run. Directionality relative to trend matters. The bull's equivalence argument fails on this basis.

## Rebuttal: Bull's Own Math Concedes the Point

The bull models $2.8B+ net income/quarter at $14–15B revenue. Forward EPS consensus is $13.08 ($3.27/quarter). Even the bull's own model needs revenue above $15B OR margin expansion above current levels to reach consensus. The bull calls it "stretch, not fantasy." Paying 156× TTM P/E for a "stretch" scenario is the exact risk. Misses at these multiples are punished severely.

## Rebuttal: Market Participation Still Requires Share Growth

To get AMD Data Center from $3.7B/quarter to $8–10B/quarter (needed for Forward EPS), AMD must capture increasing market share. The AI market growing 30–40% doesn't get AMD there without share gain — Nvidia is also growing with that market while announcing new market expansions. The math doesn't work with market participation alone.

## Rebuttal: China Concession Acknowledged

The bull says AMD has "less" China exposure than Nvidia — not zero. That is an acknowledgment China risk is real. Senate hearings underway. Export restrictions tightening. AMD revenue from China will be impacted. Both sides agree; the bear treats it as significant risk, the bull minimizes it.

## Rebuttal: Operating Margin Ranging, Not Trending

Q3'25: 13.7% → Q4'25: 17.1% → Q1'26: 14.4% — ranging between 13–17%, not consistently expanding. AMD needs to push toward 20–25% margins to justify Forward EPS. No evidence of a locked-in trajectory to those levels.

## Closing Bear Argument

NOT saying: AMD is a bad company. Revenue growing, FCF compounding, AI tailwind real.

IS saying: At $466, after a distribution day following a +39% parabolic run, with:
- 4.4× (or bull's own 2.8–3.27×) EPS ramp required
- Sequential margin compression
- Inventory +20.5% in four quarters  
- Every senior executive selling into all-time highs
- China export risk growing
- Data center cancellation narrative circulating
- No confirmed bottom

The risk/reward does NOT favor new entry. Mean analyst target: $482.69 = 3.5% upside. Downside to May base ($420–$424): -9.9%. The asymmetry is closer to 1:1 or worse, not the 3:1 the bull claims.

**AMD is HOLD for existing longs. No new money at $466. Wait for confirmed support at $420–$449.**
