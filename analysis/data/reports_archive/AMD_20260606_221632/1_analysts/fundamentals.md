# Fundamentals Analyst Report — AMD — 2026-06-06

## Overview

Advanced Micro Devices (AMD) is a semiconductor company competing across CPUs, GPUs, FPGAs, and adaptive computing. The company has undergone a dramatic financial transformation driven by AI compute demand and strong data center GPU adoption. As of 2026-06-06, AMD trades at $466.38 with a market cap of $760.48B.

---

## Valuation

### P/E (TTM): 156.5×
AMD's trailing P/E of 156.5× is extremely elevated by any historical standard. At EPS TTM of $2.98 and a $466 stock price, the market is pricing in massive earnings growth. The critical question: is the growth rate sufficient to justify this multiple?

**Context:** AMD's EPS TTM is $2.98, but the **Forward EPS is $13.08** — representing a projected **4.4× earnings increase** from TTM to forward estimates. If AMD delivers $13.08 in forward EPS, the Forward P/E at today's price would be **35.67×** — a reasonable multiple for a high-growth semiconductor company in a structural AI upcycle.

**Verdict:** The TTM P/E is misleading because it reflects a transition period. The Forward P/E of 35.67× is the relevant multiple and is reasonable if AMD executes on its growth roadmap.

### PEG Ratio: 1.12
With a PEG of 1.12 (just above 1.0), AMD is priced at roughly growth-neutral territory. PEG below 1 = undervalued relative to growth; above 1 = slight premium. At 1.12, AMD is not egregiously overvalued on a growth-adjusted basis — this supports the bull case that despite the high P/E, the growth rate largely justifies the multiple.

### Price/Book: 11.79×
At $466 / $39.55 book value = 11.79× P/B. This is rich but expected for a high-growth tech company. Tangible book value per share is far lower (book is inflated by $41.5B in goodwill and intangibles from the Xilinx acquisition). Tangible book is ~$14.10/share, giving a P/Tangible Book of ~33×.

### EV/EBITDA: 101.2×
This is the most alarming valuation metric. At EV/EBITDA of 101.2×, AMD is pricing in extraordinary future cash generation. For reference, mature semiconductor companies trade at 15–25× EV/EBITDA. Even high-growth AI chip names trade at 30–60×. AMD at 101× reflects either: (a) the market pricing in EBITDA growing 3–4× in 2–3 years, or (b) speculative excess.

**Verdict:** EV/EBITDA at 101× is stretched even for a high-growth AI narrative. This is the primary valuation risk.

### Analyst Consensus
- Mean target: $482.69 (3.5% above current price of $466.38)
- High target: $665.00 (42.5% upside)
- Low target: $225.00 (52% downside)
- Recommendation: STRONG_BUY

The analyst consensus mean is just 3.5% above current price after the selloff — suggesting the analyst community's base case is very close to where AMD is trading right now. The strong buy rating with a high target of $665 reflects the bull scenario, while the low of $225 reflects a full bear scenario (return to pre-AI valuation).

## Profitability

### Revenue Trajectory
| Quarter | Revenue | QoQ Change |
|---------|---------|-----------|
| Q2'25   | $7.685B | — |
| Q3'25   | $9.246B | +20.3% |
| Q4'25   | $10.270B | +11.1% |
| Q1'26   | $10.253B | -0.2% |

Revenue growth has been explosive through 2025 — +33.4% from Q2'25 to Q1'26. The Q1'26 sequential flat (-0.2%) vs. Q4'25 is worth monitoring. This could reflect seasonal patterns or early signs of AI data center demand normalization. The annual TTM revenue is $37.45B.

### Gross Margin: 53.1%
AMD's gross margin of 53.1% is healthy for a fabless semiconductor company. For context:
- Q1'26 gross profit $5.416B on $10.253B revenue = 52.8%
- Q4'25 gross profit $5.577B on $10.270B revenue = 54.3%
- Q3'25 gross profit $4.780B on $9.246B revenue = 51.7%

Margins are improving in the right direction, though Q4'25 to Q1'26 showed a modest dip. The 53% level is below AMD's longer-term target (55%+) but reflects the current mix shift as high-margin GPU business scales.

### Operating Margin: 14.4%
The operating margin of 14.4% is somewhat disappointing relative to the revenue scale, primarily because of heavy R&D investment:
- Q1'26 R&D: $2.397B (23.4% of revenue)
- Q1'26 SG&A: $1.253B (12.2% of revenue)

Combined OpEx ratio: ~35.6% of revenue. This is AMD investing aggressively in RDNA 5, CDNA 4, and MI-series next-gen GPU development to compete with NVDA. Operating leverage should improve as revenue scales faster than R&D growth.

**Q1'26 Operating Income: $1.476B** — down from Q4'25's $1.752B. This sequential decline in operating income (on roughly flat revenue) is a flag worth noting — OpEx is growing faster than revenue in Q1'26.

### Profit Margin: 13.4%
Net profit margin of 13.4% is solid for a growth company in heavy investment mode. Q1'26 net income of $1.383B vs. Q4'25 $1.511B shows sequential decline — consistent with the operating income trend.

### ROE: 8.1% / ROA: 3.6%
Both metrics appear low for a $760B market cap company, reflecting the massive goodwill/intangibles on the balance sheet from the Xilinx acquisition ($41.5B). Strip out goodwill and the returns look substantially better. ROE on tangible equity would be approximately: $4.93B TTM net income / $22.96B tangible equity = ~21.5% — much more impressive.

## Balance Sheet

### Cash Position: $12.35B (Q1'26)
AMD's cash and short-term investments have grown from $5.867B (Q2'25) to $12.347B (Q1'26) — more than doubling in three quarters. This is exceptional cash generation and positions AMD strongly for:
- R&D investment without dilution
- Potential acquisitions
- Share buybacks (already executing: $355M in Q1'26)
- Debt paydown

### Debt: $3.871B (minimal)
D/E ratio of 6.00 is somewhat misleading because it includes the massive Xilinx intangibles in the equity denominator. Net cash position: $12.347B cash - $3.871B debt = **$8.476B net cash** — AMD has a fortress balance sheet. Debt is minimal and easily serviceable.

### Current Ratio: 2.73
Working capital of $18.1B vs. a current ratio of 2.73× indicates AMD has ample liquidity to meet near-term obligations. This is not a balance sheet risk.

### Inventory: $8.045B (Q1'26)
Inventory has grown from $6.677B (Q2'25) to $8.045B (Q1'26) — a 20.5% increase. This warrants attention. Rising inventory can reflect:
- **Bullish:** AMD is building inventory to meet anticipated AI GPU demand
- **Bearish:** AMD is building inventory because demand is softer than expected

Given the sequential revenue plateau in Q1'26 (+$0B QoQ) alongside rising inventory, this is worth monitoring in Q2'26 results.

### Goodwill + Intangibles: $41.498B
This represents 52.1% of total assets — the lingering footprint of the Xilinx acquisition. These intangibles do not generate cash directly and will amortize over time, creating a drag on GAAP earnings. The gap between GAAP EPS ($2.98 TTM) and Forward EPS ($13.08) partly reflects this amortization schedule unwinding and true earnings power emerging.

## Cash Flow

### Free Cash Flow: $7.17B (TTM)
AMD's FCF generation is outstanding and accelerating:
| Quarter | Operating CF | Capex | FCF |
|---------|-------------|-------|-----|
| Q2'25   | $2.011B | -$282M | $1.729B |
| Q3'25   | $2.159B | -$258M | $1.901B |
| Q4'25   | $2.600B | -$222M | $2.378B |
| Q1'26   | $2.955B | -$389M | $2.566B |

FCF grew 48.4% from Q2'25 to Q1'26. At this trajectory, AMD could generate $10–12B in annual FCF in FY26 — implying a FCF yield of 1.3–1.6% at current market cap. This is a modest yield for a high-growth company.

**Capital intensity:** Capex jumped from $222M (Q4'25) to $389M (Q1'26), a 75% sequential increase. AMD is investing more heavily in infrastructure. Despite this, FCF still grew. AMD's fabless model means capex is relatively low compared to IDM competitors.

### Share Buybacks
AMD returned $355M via buybacks in Q1'26, $160M in Q4'25, $460M in Q3'25, $524M in Q2'25. The buyback program is not aggressive at scale ($1.5B over 4 quarters vs. $7.17B FCF), suggesting AMD is prioritizing reinvestment and cash preservation.

## Growth

### EPS Trajectory: The Most Important Metric
- EPS TTM: $2.98 (includes Xilinx amortization drag, transition period)
- Forward EPS: $13.08 (implies massive earnings inflection)

The market is pricing a **4.4× EPS increase** from TTM to forward consensus. This reflects analyst expectations of:
1. Data center GPU revenue continuing to scale (MI-series market share gains)
2. Xilinx amortization moderating (improving GAAP margins)
3. CPU/EPYC continued market share gains vs. Intel
4. Operating leverage from current heavy R&D investment paying off

If AMD delivers $13.08 in EPS, the stock at $466.38 trades at **35.7× forward earnings** — attractive for a company growing at this pace.

**Risk:** If AI data center demand slows, GPU mix normalizes, or Intel/NVDA competition intensifies, Forward EPS estimates will be revised down. A 20% cut to forward EPS (to ~$10.50) would push Forward P/E to ~44× at today's price — still defensible but less compelling.

## Financial Health Assessment

**Overall: Strong, with caution on sequential Q1'26 trends**

Strengths:
- $8.5B net cash position (fortress balance sheet)
- FCF growing strongly ($2.566B in Q1'26)
- Revenue TTM $37.45B growing rapidly
- Manageable debt ($3.87B)
- Analyst strong buy consensus

Cautions:
- Q1'26 operating income declined sequentially ($1.476B vs. Q4'25 $1.752B)
- Inventory rising 20.5% over 3 quarters (demand vs. build-ahead ambiguity)
- R&D at 23.4% of revenue — necessary but limits near-term margins
- EV/EBITDA of 101× is stretched; dependent on EBITDA growing 3–4× to justify
- Heavy insider selling (CEO Su sold $55.7M in shares at May peak)
