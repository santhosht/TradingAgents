### Company & Sector News

**1. Broadcom (AVGO) Guidance Disappointment — June 4**
*What happened:* Broadcom reported higher revenue for its latest quarter with accelerating AI chip demand but issued forward guidance that fell short of investor expectations. Shares tumbled 13% in a single session ($285B market cap rout per FT Markets headline).

*Why it matters for AMD:* This is the most important near-term risk event for AMD. Broadcom's miss triggered a sector-wide selloff — "Intel, AMD, Micron shares trim losses after Broadcom results spark semiconductor sector sell-off." The AVGO guidance failure does three things to AMD: (a) It resets the AI capex spending narrative — investors now question whether hyperscaler AI chip orders are front-loaded; (b) It increases valuation risk across all AI-adjacent semis, including AMD whose forward P/E of 40× is pricing in continued earnings acceleration; (c) It creates a narrative headwind even if AMD's own book of business (MI300X with Azure/AWS/GCP) is unaffected. AVGO's miss was on its custom AI chip business — not commodity compute — but markets rarely distinguish in the immediate selloff.

*Direction:* **Negative** — sector contagion, valuation risk, narrative headwind

---

**2. TSMC CEO Confirms AI Chip Demand Exceeds Supply for Years — June 4**
*What happened:* At its annual shareholders' meeting, TSMC CEO C.C. Wei stated the company won't be able to fulfill demand over the next few years even as new manufacturing capacity comes online.

*Why it matters for AMD:* AMD is a fabless company — it manufactures through TSMC. TSMC CEO's demand confirmation is direct, first-hand supply-chain evidence that AI chip orders (including AMD's MI300X, EPYC, and upcoming MI350/MI400) are real, contracted, and multi-year. This is not an analyst estimate — it is the foundry CEO speaking about actual production orders. For AMD's bull thesis, this is the strongest possible fundamental data point available short of AMD's own earnings.

*Direction:* **Strong Positive** — confirms AI demand structural durability, directly validates AMD's MI300X revenue ramp narrative

---

**3. Trump Officials Worried U.S. Loophole Let Chinese Firms Buy Nvidia Blackwell Chips — June 5**
*What happened:* Bloomberg reports that Trump officials have spent the past week debating whether their China tech policy has been effectively enforced, with concern that Chinese firms accessed Nvidia's latest Blackwell GPUs through loopholes.

*Why it matters for AMD:* Dual-direction impact. Near-term negative: any tightening of export controls will increase scrutiny on all AI chip sales to China, potentially limiting AMD's MI300X addressable market in that region. Longer-term potential positive: if Nvidia is increasingly blocked from China while AMD operates under different licensing rules, AMD could gain share. However, historical pattern suggests export restriction tightening hurts all U.S. chip companies simultaneously. The policy uncertainty itself suppresses valuation multiples for the sector.

*Direction:* **Mildly Negative** — export control uncertainty is a valuation overhang for all U.S. AI chip companies

**4. Europe's Best-Performing AI Stocks of 2026 — June 5 (Euronews)**
*What happened:* European AI infrastructure companies — optical chips, testing equipment, servers — have delivered gains up to 2,200% in 2026.

*Why it matters for AMD:* This confirms the global AI infrastructure super-cycle is not a U.S.-only phenomenon. European semiconductor exposure companies are experiencing similar tailwinds. For AMD, this validates the thesis that AI infrastructure demand is a multi-year, geographically diverse build-out — not a single hyperscaler capex surge.

*Direction:* **Positive** — macro validation of global AI infrastructure demand cycle

### Macro News

**5. May Jobs Report — Friday June 6, 8:30am ET**
*What happened:* The May non-farm payrolls report will be released Friday morning. ADP private payrolls for May came in at 122,000 — stronger than expected. Job openings in April surged to 7.6 million, the highest in nearly two years.

*Why it matters for AMD:* The jobs report is the dominant macro binary catalyst for Friday. The ADP beat and the Job Openings surge create a complex setup:
- Strong jobs = Fed less likely to cut rates in near-term = higher discount rates = compressed growth stock multiples = AMD valuation pressure
- Weak jobs = recession fear + defensive rotation = tech sells off broadly
- "Goldilocks" (moderate jobs) = tech rally resumes

The ADP 122k print (stronger than expected) combined with JOLTs at 7.6M (a near 2-year high) modestly increases the probability of a "hawkish" jobs print on Friday. This is a headwind for AMD specifically because AMD's $853B market cap is justified almost entirely by discounted future earnings — rising rates compress that more than any near-term fundamental.

*Direction:* **Binary / Uncertain** — ADP and JOLTs lean hawkish which is a headwind for rate-sensitive growth stocks

---

**6. Iran War Energy Costs + Euro Zone Inflation — June 4-5**
*What happened:* U.S.-Iran ceasefire is fraying; energy costs rising. Average U.S. household paying $450 more on gas/energy. Euro zone inflation hit 3.2% driven by Iran war energy pass-through.

*Why it matters for AMD:* Sustained inflation reduces the probability of near-term Fed rate cuts. Higher-for-longer rates directly compress AMD's 40× forward multiple. For a stock priced at 175× TTM P/E with forward earnings dependent on AI infrastructure spending, the macro rate environment is a critical valuation driver. Energy inflation also increases operational costs for hyperscaler data centers (AMD's primary customers) which could reduce their capex budgets.

*Direction:* **Negative** — macro inflationary pressure, rate-cut expectations pushed further out

---

**7. U.S. Proposes Fresh Tariffs on 60 Economies — June 4-5**
*What happened:* U.S. proposing new tariffs on 60 economies over forced labor trade practices.

*Why it matters for AMD:* AMD's supply chain is global — TSMC fabs in Taiwan, assembly in Malaysia, customers across Asia and Europe. Broader tariff escalation increases costs and creates supply chain uncertainty. Not an immediate AMD-specific risk, but adds to the macro uncertainty overhang that weighs on growth stock valuations.

*Direction:* **Mildly Negative** — supply chain/cost uncertainty; not acute but additive to macro headwinds
