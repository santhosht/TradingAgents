### 1. Valuation

**TTM P/E: 175.57** — This is not a valuation investors are using for AMD decisions. It reflects the trailing EPS of $2.98 which was heavily distorted by a weak Q2'25 ($0.54 EPS). It is presented for completeness only — no serious analyst is valuing AMD on trailing earnings.

**Forward P/E: 40.22** — This is the operative multiple. It prices AMD at $523.20 against Forward EPS of **$13.01**. That $13.01 represents a **4.37× jump from TTM EPS of $2.98** — implying AMD needs to multiply annual earnings from ~$4.9B to ~$21.5B in the next 12 months. This is the central valuation risk: the forward multiple is already compressed to 40× but the underlying forward earnings estimate is extraordinarily ambitious.

To achieve $13.01 forward EPS at 1.65B diluted shares, AMD needs ~$21.5B in net income. At Q1'26's 13.4% net margin on $10.25B revenue, that requires revenue of ~$160B annually — more than 4× current run rate. This suggests Forward EPS consensus is pricing in a step-change in margins (operating leverage from AI chip mix) rather than just revenue growth. Even at 25% net margins on $85B revenue, AMD needs to more than double its revenue.

**This is not necessarily impossible** — the MI300X ramp, EPYC server CPU gains, and AI PC penetration could drive significant revenue acceleration. But it means the 40× forward P/E is only "reasonable" if you believe in a transformative earnings inflection, not incremental growth.

**PEG: 1.31** — At face value, this suggests the P/E is justified by the growth rate. However, PEG ratios using forward consensus estimates on hyperbolically high growth projections can be misleading. The 1.31 PEG is reasonable only if the forward EPS materializes.

**Price/Book: 13.23** — Elevated but expected for an asset-light fabless semiconductor company with significant intangible assets (goodwill $25.3B, other intangibles $16.2B from Xilinx acquisition). Tangible book value is $22.96B; tangible book multiple is ~37×. Aggressive.

**EV/EBITDA: 113.68** — On current TTM EBITDA of $7.43B against a market cap of $853B (plus $3.87B debt, less $12.35B cash ≈ EV ~$844B), this is 113.7×. Even using Q1'26 annualized EBITDA ($2.398B × 4 = $9.59B), the multiple is ~88×. By traditional semiconductor valuation metrics, this is expensive. Justified only by the growth inflection thesis.

### 2. Profitability

**Revenue trajectory (quarterly):**
- Q2'25: $7.685B
- Q3'25: $9.246B (+20.3% QoQ)
- Q4'25: $10.270B (+11.1% QoQ)
- Q1'26: $10.253B (-0.2% QoQ) ← sequential stall

**Revenue grew 33.4% from Q2'25 to Q1'26 year-over-year basis but is now sequentially flat.** The question for Q2'26 is whether revenue re-accelerates or consolidates. The MI300X ramp and EPYC Gen 5 (Turin) are the growth levers.

**Gross Margin: 53.1%** — Healthy for fabless model. AMD's gross margins have been expanding as the product mix shifts toward higher-margin AI accelerators (MI300X) vs. lower-margin commodity GPUs. If MI300X shipments continue to grow as a percentage of revenue, further gross margin expansion to 55-58% is possible.

**Operating Margin: 14.4%** — Low relative to gross margin. The gap is explained by aggressive R&D spending:
- Q1'26 R&D: $2.397B (23.4% of revenue)
- Q4'25 R&D: $2.330B (22.7% of revenue)
- Q3'25 R&D: $2.139B (23.1% of revenue)

AMD is accelerating R&D investment — up 26.5% YoY in Q1'26. This is the right strategic move (MI350, MI400, EPYC Zen 5 development) but it structurally suppresses operating margins in the near term. The operating leverage thesis requires these R&D investments to generate product cycles that materially raise ASPs and volumes.

**EPS sequential decline: $0.92 (Q4'25) → $0.84 (Q1'26) = -8.7%** — This is the most important fundamental concern. Revenue was essentially flat QoQ (-0.2%) while EPS fell -8.7%. The gap is explained by:
- R&D increased $67M QoQ → ~$0.04/share impact
- SG&A increased $55M QoQ → ~$0.03/share impact
- Net interest income swung negative (Q4 had $179M interest income vs Q1 -$37M net interest expense)

The sequential EPS decline in the context of flat revenue must reverse in Q2'26 or the operating leverage narrative is at risk.

**ROE: 8.1% | ROA: 3.6%** — These are modest returns for a company trading at 13× book. They reflect the current earnings level, not the forward. If Forward EPS of $13.01 materializes, ROE would jump to approximately 33% — transforming the valuation picture entirely.

### 3. Balance Sheet

**Cash: $12.35B | Total Debt: $3.87B | Net Cash: $8.48B**

AMD has a **net cash positive balance sheet** — a significant strength. The $12.35B cash position provides ample runway for R&D investment, potential M&A, and withstanding a demand cycle correction. Short-term investments of $6.762B add additional liquidity.

**D/E Ratio: ~6%** ($3.87B debt / $64.46B equity) — Very low leverage. AMD is not a levered story. The long-term debt of $2.35B matures well into the future and is easily serviced by $2.566B/quarter FCF.

**Current Ratio: 2.73** — Healthy liquidity. Current assets of $28.63B vs current liabilities of $10.51B provides a comfortable buffer.

**Goodwill and Intangibles: $41.5B** ($25.34B goodwill + $16.15B other intangibles) from the Xilinx acquisition. This represents 52% of total assets. While currently not at impairment risk (Xilinx IP is being deployed in AI accelerators), any major product failure or competitive displacement of Xilinx-based IP would trigger impairment charges.

**Inventory: $8.045B** (Q1'26 vs $7.920B Q4'25) — Inventory is building (+$125M QoQ, WIP $4.748B). This could reflect preparation for demand ramp (positive) or building against uncertainty (neutral-to-negative). WIP dominance suggests active manufacturing pipeline.

### 4. Cash Flow

- Q2'25: $1.729B
- Q3'25: $1.901B
- Q4'25: $2.378B
- Q1'26: **$2.566B** ← new quarterly high

FCF is accelerating — up 48.5% from Q2'25 to Q1'26. This is the most unambiguously positive fundamental in the dataset. AMD is converting revenue growth into real cash with improving efficiency. Even at $2.566B/quarter, the annualized FCF of ~$10.3B would imply an FCF yield of ~1.2% on the current $853B market cap. Very low FCF yield — typical of high-growth tech — but the direction is right.

**Operating cash flow: $2.955B** (Q1'26) — The highest in the series. The gap between operating CF ($2.955B) and FCF ($2.566B) is the $389M capex spend. Capex is rising but is disciplined (only 3.8% of revenue vs. peers who invest 10-15% in fabs). The fabless model is the reason AMD can generate high FCF while investing heavily in R&D.
