## Round 1 — Opening Bull Case

Let me lay out why AMD at $523 is a BUY, not a sell, despite the surface-level concerns.

**The TSMC confirmation is the most important fundamental data point this week.** Not AVGO's guidance, not the jobs report — the TSMC CEO saying, on record, at the annual shareholder meeting: *"We won't be able to fulfill demand over the next few years, even as more manufacturing capacity comes online."* That is not an analyst estimate. That is the CEO of AMD's primary foundry partner telling you — from actual production orders — that AI chip demand is real, contracted, and multi-year. AMD's MI300X is manufactured by TSMC. The order backlog the CEO is referencing includes AMD's chips.

**The MI300X is in production at scale across all three major hyperscalers.** Microsoft Azure is running MI300X for AI inference. Google Cloud has deployed it. AWS is using it for training workloads. These are not pilot programs — they are production deployments. The revenue from these deployments is what drove AMD's revenue from $7.685B (Q2'25) to $10.253B (Q1'26) — a 33.4% jump in three quarters. That demand doesn't evaporate because AVGO missed its guidance number.

**AVGO's miss is irrelevant to AMD's order book.** Here's the distinction the bears are missing: Broadcom makes custom ASICs for specific hyperscaler customers (Google's TPUs, Meta's MTIA). These are bespoke chips for single customers. AMD makes general-purpose AI accelerators that any hyperscaler can deploy. When Google over-orders custom Broadcom chips and then pulls back, it affects Broadcom specifically — not AMD. AMD's MI300X is a catalogue product that hyperscalers can deploy flexibly. Different market, different dynamics.

**FCF is accelerating.** $2.566B in Q1'26 — the highest quarterly FCF in AMD's history. Not $2.0B, not $2.3B — $2.566B. Up from $1.729B just three quarters ago. This is a company generating real cash at an accelerating rate. The bulls aren't asking you to trust forward projections — the cash is already flowing.

**EPYC is a structural win, not a cyclical one.** AMD's EPYC Turin (Zen 5) server CPUs are gaining share from Intel Xeon in data centers. This is not AI hype — this is AMD winning on performance per watt and price per thread. Server CPU market share gains are sticky and durable. Even if the AI chip cycle cools, EPYC revenue continues growing.

**The PEG of 1.31 is reasonable.** A PEG below 1.5 for a company growing earnings this rapidly is not obviously overpriced. Yes, the forward EPS of $13.01 requires execution. But AMD has beaten EPS estimates in 8 consecutive quarters. They have a history of delivering.

At $523, with TSMC confirming multi-year demand, with $2.566B quarterly FCF, with three hyperscaler production deployments, AMD is not a sell. It is a disciplined accumulation opportunity.

## Round 2 — Bull Rebuttal + New Arguments

The bear made three points I need to address directly: the analyst consensus target ($482.69) being below price, the sequential EPS decline, and the insider selling pattern. Let me take each one.

**On analyst targets being below price:** This happens every time a stock undergoes a legitimate re-rating. Analysts use models — DCF, forward P/E comps — and their models have inertia. When AMD was at $200 in January 2026, the consensus target was probably $220. When it hit $350, the target was $300. The target chases the price, not the other way around. The fact that the current mean target is $482.69 simply means analysts haven't updated their models fast enough. The $665 high target — held by at least one analyst who has done the updated work — is what AMD can achieve on the bull case. The mean target is a lagging indicator, not a forecast.

**On the sequential EPS decline:** The bear is arithmetically correct — $0.92 to $0.84 is -8.7%. But look at what drove it. R&D spending in Q1'26 was $2.397B — $67M more than Q4'25. SG&A was up $55M. Net interest income swung from +$179M to -$37M (a $216M swing) due to money market changes. These are not operational deterioration signals. They are the investment phase of a growth company. AMD is spending $2.4B per quarter on R&D building the MI350 and MI400 — next-generation chips that will command even higher ASPs than the MI300X. Companies that under-invest in R&D to protect short-term EPS end up with stale products. AMD is making the right call.

**On insider selling:** CEO diversification at this level of wealth concentration is standard financial planning — not a signal that the company is fundamentally broken. Lisa Su has been at AMD for over a decade. Her net worth is overwhelmingly concentrated in AMD stock and options. Selling $72M out of a position worth hundreds of millions is not unusual for an executive in her position. If she had sold 100% of her holdings, that would be a signal. She has not.

**New point — EPYC Turin's competitive position is stronger than the data shows.** The Q1'26 revenue data captures only the early ramp of Turin. Intel is struggling with its competing Granite Rapids platform. When server refresh cycles accelerate in H2'26, AMD is positioned to take an additional 5-10 percentage points of server CPU market share. Each point of server CPU market share is approximately $500M-700M in additional annual revenue.

**New point — AMD AI PC is not in this revenue yet.** The Ryzen AI 300 series for AI PCs is beginning to ship. This is the third revenue vector (alongside data center GPU and server CPU) that is not yet visible in the financial statements. The AI PC refresh cycle (2026-2028) could add $3-5B in incremental annual revenue.

The bear case requires you to believe that TSMC is wrong about demand, that hyperscalers will cancel MI300X orders, and that EPYC stops winning. None of these are evidenced by the data.
