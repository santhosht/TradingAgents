## Round 2 — Bear Rebuttal + New Arguments

The bull made some fair points on the analyst lag and R&D investment thesis. Let me engage with those honestly before making new arguments.

**On analyst targets lagging:** The bull is right that analyst targets chase price — this is a well-documented phenomenon. But the argument cuts both ways. If analyst targets are always chasing price, then when price falls, targets lag on the downside too — creating a false sense of support. The current target of $482.69 was probably set when AMD was much lower. As AMD overran the target, analysts may have been reluctant to raise targets without evidence of the forward EPS materializing. If Q2'26 shows another sequential EPS miss, those targets will be *cut*, not raised. The bull is assuming analysts will raise targets. I'm saying analysts will raise targets only if the earnings materialize — and Q1'26 suggests the earnings are not materializing on the expected trajectory.

**On R&D as the EPS drag:** The bull says R&D is investment, not deterioration. Let me test this. R&D increased $67M QoQ in Q1'26. At 1.65B diluted shares, that's ~$0.04/share. Net interest swung negative by $216M — that's ~$0.13/share. So R&D and interest income changes explain ~$0.17/share out of the $0.08/share EPS decline... but wait, EPS only fell $0.08. This means even setting aside R&D and interest, underlying operational performance *improved*. The bull's narrative is actually stronger than the headline number suggests. Fine — I'll concede EPS quality was decent. But this changes nothing about the forward EPS problem: $0.84/quarter × 4 = $3.36 TTM run rate. Forward consensus is $13.01. That is a 3.9× earnings jump required. The R&D will convert to products eventually — but "eventually" is not in the next 12 months.

**New point — The TSMC confirmation is not AMD-specific.** When TSMC CEO says demand exceeds supply, he is talking about the entire AI chip complex — Nvidia's H100, H200, B100; AMD's MI300X; custom ASICs for Google, Meta, Amazon. AMD is competing for TSMC's constrained capacity with Nvidia, which has more leverage over TSMC (larger share of TSMC's advanced node capacity). TSMC confirming demand helps Nvidia more than AMD because Nvidia is allocating more of that constrained capacity.

**New point — Forward EPS of $13.01 requires a margin profile AMD has never demonstrated.** Let me show the math: $13.01 EPS × 1.65B shares = $21.47B net income. At 13.4% current net margin, AMD needs $160B in revenue. At an aggressive 25% net margin (which AMD has never achieved), AMD needs $86B in revenue — more than double current levels. In two years? The current quarterly run rate is $10.25B = $41B annual. AMD would need to more than double revenue in 18-24 months while simultaneously expanding margins. Possible? Theoretically. Priced in already? Yes. Which means if AMD delivers "merely" $8-10 forward EPS instead of $13.01, the stock re-rates down to 30× $9 = $270. That is 48% downside from current price.
