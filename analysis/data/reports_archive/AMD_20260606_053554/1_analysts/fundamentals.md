# Fundamentals Analyst Report — AMD
**Run ID:** AMD_20260606_053554 | **Date:** 2026-06-06

## Overview

Advanced Micro Devices (AMD) is a semiconductor company operating across the CPU (client + server) and GPU (data center AI + gaming) segments. The company's fundamental trajectory has undergone a dramatic transformation since 2023, driven by data center GPU adoption (MI300X/MI300A) and ongoing EPYC server CPU market share gains. The most recent completed quarter is Q1 2026.

---

## 1. Valuation

### P/E TTM (156.50×)
The trailing P/E of 156.50× is by any traditional metric extreme. At $466.38 per share and EPS (TTM) of $2.98, the market is paying 156× for current earnings. This is not a value stock — it is a pure growth story priced for exponential earnings acceleration.

**Is it justified?** Only if Forward EPS is correct. Forward EPS of $13.08 implies a **339% YoY earnings jump** from TTM EPS of $2.98. At Forward P/E of 35.67×, the stock trades at a far more reasonable multiple on projected 2026 earnings. The key question is whether management and analysts are right that EPS will more than triple.

### Forward P/E (35.67×) and PEG (1.12)
Forward P/E of 35.67× is aggressive for a semiconductor company but defensible if AMD sustains 25–35% revenue growth. The PEG ratio of 1.12 suggests the market is roughly fairly valuing AMD relative to its growth rate — not egregiously expensive on a growth-adjusted basis, but not cheap either.

**PEG interpretation:** PEG ~1.0 is considered "fairly valued" by growth investors. AMD's 1.12 says: the growth rate barely justifies the forward multiple. Any growth deceleration tips this toward overvalued immediately.

### Price/Book (11.79×)
Book value per share is $39.55 (total equity $64.46B / ~1.63B shares). At $466.38, P/B = 11.79×. This is elevated, but tangible book is far more revealing: tangible book value is $22.96B, so tangible P/B ≈ 33×. The gap ($41.5B goodwill + intangibles from the Xilinx acquisition) means AMD is largely priced on intangible growth assets, not hard assets.

### EV/EBITDA (101.21×)
At 101× EV/EBITDA, AMD is valued at a level typical of high-growth SaaS companies, not traditional semiconductor manufacturers. This reflects the market's bet that AMD is transforming from a cyclical chip company into an AI infrastructure play. EBITDA of $7.43B on a TTM basis with Q1 2026 quarterly EBITDA of $2.40B running at an annualized rate of ~$9.6B — so the NTM EV/EBITDA is somewhat more manageable but still elevated at ~78×.

**Summary on Valuation:** AMD is expensive on backward-looking metrics and approximately fair on forward metrics — but only if the earnings acceleration materializes. The margin of safety is thin.

Revenue has more than tripled from the trough lows and crossed $10B quarterly — a milestone that validates AMD's AI GPU penetration story. The quarter-over-quarter deceleration (Q1 2026 flat vs Q4 2025) is the first red flag in what has been relentless sequential growth. This does not yet signal a reversal, but momentum investors will note the plateau.

**TTM Revenue: $37.45B** — this compares to approximately $22B in FY2023, confirming the transformational scale-up is real and not hypothetical.

### Gross Margin (53.1%)
Gross margin of 53.1% on a TTM basis is strong for a fabless semiconductor. Q4 2025 was particularly strong at $5.577B gross profit on $10.270B revenue = 54.3% gross margin. Q1 2026 slightly compressed to $5.416B / $10.253B = 52.8%. The trajectory shows healthy, expanding margins as the data center GPU mix (higher ASP, higher margin) grows as a share of total revenue.

### Operating Margin (14.4% TTM)
Operating margin at 14.4% TTM is relatively thin for a company with AMD's revenue scale. This reflects:
- Massive and growing R&D spend: $2.397B in Q1 2026 alone (23.4% of revenue) — AMD is investing heavily in next-gen chips
- SG&A of $1.253B in Q1 2026 (12.2% of revenue)

Q1 2026 operating income: $1.476B on $10.253B revenue = 14.4%. This is improving — Q2 2025 showed a deeply negative operating income (-$134M) from which AMD has recovered dramatically.

## 3. Balance Sheet — Very Healthy

### Cash Position
Cash and short-term investments: **$12.35B** — a major liquidity buffer that has increased sharply from $5.87B in Q2 2025. AMD's cash generation is accelerating as the AI GPU cycle matures. The $4.49B cash build in a single year (Q2 2025 to Q1 2026) is remarkable.

### Debt
Total debt: **$3.87B** — flat and declining as a percentage of assets. Net cash position: $12.35B - $3.87B = **$8.48B net cash**. AMD is a net cash company, which eliminates refinancing risk even in a rising rate environment.

**D/E ratio of 6.0** appears alarming but is misleading — the $3.87B debt against $64.46B equity actually implies a very low leverage ratio. The D/E is inflated because reported equity includes all intangible goodwill; the ratio is likely calculated differently in the data source.

### Current Ratio (2.73)
Current ratio of 2.73 indicates AMD can cover current liabilities 2.73× with current assets. Working capital is $18.1B, healthy and growing. No near-term liquidity risk.

### Goodwill and Intangibles ($41.5B)
The $41.5B goodwill and intangible assets (primarily from the $49B Xilinx acquisition in 2022) is the largest balance sheet risk. If AMD's AI GPU thesis fails or Xilinx synergies disappoint, a goodwill impairment charge would significantly damage reported equity. This risk is not imminent but structurally present.

## 4. Cash Flow — Exceptional and Accelerating

| Quarter | Operating CF | CapEx | Free Cash Flow |
|---------|-------------|-------|---------------|
| Q2 2025 | $2.011B | -$282M | $1.729B |
| Q3 2025 | $2.159B | -$258M | $1.901B |
| Q4 2025 | $2.600B | -$222M | $2.378B |
| Q1 2026 | $2.955B | -$389M | $2.566B |
| **TTM** | **$9.725B** | **-$1.151B** | **$8.574B** |

Note: The fundamentals overview reports FCF TTM as $7.17B, likely using a slightly different calculation or normalization. Using raw quarterly data, annualized FCF is $2.566B × 4 ≈ $10.26B run-rate. Either way, AMD is generating extraordinary free cash flow.

AMD is a **fabless model** — it designs chips and outsources manufacturing to TSMC. CapEx of $389M in Q1 2026 is therefore minimal (mostly data centers, test equipment, design tools) and growing slowly relative to revenue. The fabless model's low capital intensity means FCF is structurally high.

**FCF yield on market cap:** $7.17B TTM FCF / $760B market cap = **0.94% FCF yield** — low by traditional value standards, but consistent with a high-growth company being valued on future cash flows rather than current.

### Buybacks
AMD is returning capital modestly: $355M in Q1 2026 buybacks, $524M in Q2 2025. At this rate (~$1.6B/year), buybacks are meaningful but not aggressive relative to the cash balance. AMD appears to be prioritizing cash accumulation for potential acquisitions or R&D investments.

## 5. Growth — The Core Investment Thesis

### EPS Acceleration: From $2.98 TTM to $13.08 Forward
This is the pivotal data point. The consensus Forward EPS of $13.08 requires AMD to deliver approximately $10.10 additional EPS in the remaining quarters of FY2026. Q1 2026 EPS was $0.84 — sustained at this rate would be $3.36 annualized, far short of $13.08.

The only way to reach $13.08 is if:
1. AMD's data center GPU revenue approximately doubles or triples in H2 2026
2. Operating leverage kicks in (as revenue scales, R&D/SG&A as % of revenue shrinks)
3. The MI400 GPU launch (or equivalent next-gen product) generates significant incremental revenue

This requires flawless execution and continued hyperscaler demand. The AVGO earnings warning (from the sector news) introduces genuine doubt about whether this ramp materializes at the projected pace.

### Revenue Growth Path
Q1 2026 revenue of $10.253B × 4 = $41B annualized. TTM is $37.45B. The market's Forward EPS of $13.08 likely prices in full-year 2026 revenue of $45–50B+, implying continued growth acceleration after the Q1 2026 plateau.

## 7. ROE and ROA

- **ROE: 8.1%** — modest for a high-P/E company; reflects the large equity base from Xilinx acquisition goodwill

These metrics will improve as earnings scale, but they currently show AMD is not yet generating exceptional returns on its capital base. This is expected at this stage of the growth cycle.

## Fundamental Summary

AMD is a financially sound, aggressively growing AI semiconductor company with exceptional cash generation but an extreme valuation that requires sustained execution to justify. Key highlights:

**Positives:**
- Revenue scale-up is real: $37.45B TTM vs ~$22B FY2023
- FCF generation excellent: $7.17B+ TTM, growing
- Net cash position: $8.48B — no financial distress risk
- Forward P/E of 35.67× reasonable if Forward EPS of $13.08 materializes
- Quarterly operating CF growing each quarter (Q2 2025: $2.01B → Q1 2026: $2.96B)
- Analyst consensus: STRONG_BUY, mean target $482.69

**Risks:**
- TTM P/E of 156× assumes massive forward earnings acceleration
- Forward EPS of $13.08 requires ~4× jump from current $2.98 TTM — execution risk is real
- Q1 2026 revenue plateaued QoQ (-0.2%) — first stall in growth momentum
- EV/EBITDA of 101× is extreme; any multiple compression is painful
- $41.5B goodwill — Xilinx impairment risk if synergies disappoint
- ROE (8.1%) and ROA (3.6%) are low for the market premium being paid
