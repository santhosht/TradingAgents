## 1. The Valuation Is Indefensible Without Flawless Execution

Let's start with the numbers the bulls will not dwell on.

**TTM P/E: 156.50×.** AMD earned $2.98 per share over the trailing twelve months. The stock trades at $466.38. You are paying 156 times actual, earned money for this stock today. That is not a growth premium — that is speculative fiction priced into a semiconductor company.

**EV/EBITDA: 101.21×.** This is SaaS-company territory. AMD is a chip company operating in a cyclical industry with a history of boom-bust demand cycles. Paying 101× EBITDA for a fabless semiconductor in a sector where one quarter of demand deceleration rewrites the entire model is reckless.

**The Forward EPS fantasy: $13.08.** The entire bull case rests on Forward EPS of $13.08, which implies a **339% jump from today's TTM EPS of $2.98**. Q1 2026 actual EPS was $0.84. Annualized, that is $3.36 — not $13.08. To reach $13.08 for full-year 2026, AMD must somehow generate approximately $10.10 in additional EPS across the remaining quarters of the year. That means H2 2026 must deliver EPS roughly **3× the annualized run rate from the most recent quarter**. This is not a growth assumption — it is a moonshot. And the AVGO guidance miss just fired a torpedo directly at the demand narrative that underpins it.

**PEG of 1.12 provides zero margin of safety.** A PEG of 1.0 is "fair value" by definition. AMD at 1.12 means: any deceleration in growth — any at all — tips the stock into overvalued territory immediately. There is no cushion. Any earnings miss, any guidance reduction, any quarterly revenue plateau immediately breaks the model.

## 2. Revenue Growth Has Already Stalled — The Market Hasn't Priced This

The single most important data point buried in the fundamentals report is this: **Q1 2026 revenue of $10.253B represents -0.2% sequential decline from Q4 2025's $10.270B.** This is the first quarter-over-quarter plateau in what had been relentless sequential growth.

Think about what this means in context. The bull case requires AMD to go from $10.25B quarterly revenue today to $45–50B+ annualized in 2026 to justify forward estimates. Q1 2026 already showed the first stall. If Q2 2026 shows another flat or negative quarter, the Forward EPS of $13.08 collapses — and with it, every price target above $300.

This is not a minor data point. Sequential deceleration at peak valuation is the single most dangerous combination in investing. AMD bulls are extrapolating the trajectory from 2023–2025 into 2026 without acknowledging that the momentum has already paused.

## 4. AVGO's Guidance Miss Is a Direct Read-Through to AMD's Demand Story

The bull community wants to dismiss the AVGO guidance miss as Broadcom-specific. This is denial.

Broadcom sells AI chips, networking silicon, and custom ASICs to the same hyperscalers — Google, Meta, Microsoft — that AMD is counting on to buy its MI-series GPUs. When Broadcom's management guides below expectations on AI infrastructure revenue, there are only three explanations:
1. Hyperscaler AI capex is decelerating
2. Broadcom is losing share to alternatives
3. Order timing shifted

Option 1 is catastrophic for AMD. Option 2 ironically suggests AMD might be gaining share — but there is zero evidence of this; AMD has no hyperscaler share gains announced in the past seven days. Option 3 is the most charitable read, but "timing shifts" have a habit of becoming permanent reductions when macro conditions tighten.

Barron's is direct: the chip selloff "isn't done yet." The market sold Micron -13%, Nvidia -6.2%, Meta -5.5%, AVGO -7.9%, and AMD -10.86% in a single session. Indiscriminate sector selloffs of this magnitude do not reverse in one or two sessions. They resolve in weeks.

## 5. Jensen Huang Just Told You AMD Is Not the Winner

On June 6, 2026 — literally today — Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang publicly identified Marvell (not AMD) as the next $1 trillion semiconductor company. The Motley Fool article's parenthetical is damning: "Hint: It's Not AMD or Sandisk."

Jensen Huang is not a neutral party — he is the direct competitor. But his statement reveals the competitive architecture that is emerging: **Nvidia for general-purpose AI compute, Broadcom/Marvell for custom ASICs.** AMD is being squeezed out of the primary AI chip conversation.

This is the structural threat the bulls consistently understate. AMD's ROCm software ecosystem remains inferior to CUDA. Every AI model trained on CUDA infrastructure makes it harder to migrate to AMD hardware. The switching costs run in Nvidia's direction. Meanwhile, hyperscalers are building custom ASICs (Google TPUs, Amazon Trainium, Microsoft Maia) that bypass both Nvidia and AMD entirely. AMD's addressable market is being compressed from both sides simultaneously: Nvidia above, custom ASICs below.

The capital rotation signal is already visible in Reddit discussions where TSMC is being discussed as the "preferred semiconductor alternative" — not AMD, not Nvidia, but the foundry that makes chips for everyone. That is not a bullish signal for AMD specifically.

## 8. Rate Environment Is Structurally Hostile to AMD's Valuation

The May jobs report was stronger than expected. ADP private payrolls: 122,000. Job openings in April: 7.6 million — highest in nearly two years. Every data point pointing to a robust labor market is a data point against rate cuts.

AMD trades at 156× TTM P/E. High-multiple stocks are duration assets — their theoretical fair value is the present value of future earnings discounted at the risk-free rate. When the risk-free rate rises, the discount rate rises, and the present value of future earnings falls. This is not opinion — it is mathematics.

If the Fed keeps rates higher for longer, AMD's forward earnings model — which requires massive 2026 EPS acceleration — becomes less valuable in present-value terms. The rate compression that expanded AMD's multiple from $100 to $546 will run in reverse. There is no scenario where higher-for-longer rates are good for a stock at 156× earnings.
