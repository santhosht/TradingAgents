## Round 2 — Bear Rebuttal

The bull's most interesting argument is that the strong jobs report helps hyperscaler capex budgets. Let me engage with this specifically.

**Hyperscaler capex decisions are made on multi-year cycles, not quarterly jobs reports.** The bull is right that Microsoft and Google don't cancel MI300X orders because the Fed delays a rate cut. But that misses the point. The question isn't whether AMD's existing MI300X orders are cancelled — it's whether AMD's stock deserves a 40× forward multiple in a higher-for-longer rate environment. It doesn't. The market's willingness to pay 40× forward for AMD was predicated on declining rates. With rates sticky, the appropriate forward multiple is 32-36×. On $13.01 EPS, that's $416-$469. Meaningful downside from $523.

**On MU's sold-out HBM:** The bull presents this as validation of MI300X demand. But consider: HBM is supply-constrained because Micron (and SK Hynix, Samsung) cannot produce it fast enough — not because AMD is ordering unlimited quantities. A supply constraint on an input component means AMD's MI300X production is also supply-constrained. Supply-constrained production is not the same as revenue acceleration. AMD can only ship as many MI300X units as TSMC can produce with available HBM. This caps upside, not expands it.

**On the "AVGO is different from AMD" argument:** The bull distinguishes AVGO's custom ASICs from AMD's general-purpose accelerators. Fair distinction. But the Barrons institutional narrative doesn't make this distinction — it sells the entire semiconductor sector together. When institutional managers reduce "AI chip exposure," they sell AMD along with AVGO and NVDA regardless of the technical differences. Market narratives don't respect fundamental distinctions during selloffs.

**On the insider selling timing:** The bull says Lisa Su sold at $433 and AMD went to $546 — proving her timing was wrong. But she has now been proven right on a longer time horizon. AMD is at $523 and falling — closer to her $433 sale price than to the $546 high. If AMD continues to $480, her sale at $433 looks prescient again. The CEO had a three-week window where the market proved her wrong. Now the market is correcting back toward her view.
