# NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) — Fundamental Analysis Report
**As of June 6, 2026**

## Executive Summary

NVIDIA is executing at a level with essentially no historical precedent for a company at this scale. The central question is not whether NVIDIA is a great business — it unambiguously is — but whether the current price already discounts the extraordinary growth trajectory. On a forward basis, NVDA looks cheap relative to its growth rate. On a trailing basis, it looks optically expensive. The quarterly data strongly supports the forward estimates.

---

## 1. Valuation

### Trailing vs. Forward P/E
- **TTM P/E: 31.36x** — Optically aggressive, but rapidly being arbitraged away by earnings growth.
- **Forward P/E: 16.17x** — Near-doubling of EPS from $6.54 (TTM) to $12.68 (forward). Not speculation — quarterly EPS progression: $1.08 → $1.30 → $1.76 → $2.39 confirms the trajectory.
- A Forward P/E of 16.17x for a company growing earnings ~94% YoY is not stretched; it is deeply discounted by conventional growth frameworks.

### PEG Ratio: 0.66
Single most important datapoint. PEG below 1.0 signals undervaluation relative to growth. A PEG of 0.66 on a $5 trillion company implies the market is not fully pricing in the earnings growth curve. Given the quarterly execution record, the stock is genuinely cheap on a growth-adjusted basis.

### Price/Book: 31.69x
Reflects asset-light, IP-driven model. Book value per share of $6.47 is low not because the company is undercapitalized (equity hit $195.5B in Q2'26) but because earnings power vastly exceeds tangible assets. Poor lens for this business model.

### EV/EBITDA: 29.75x
Backward-looking on a fast-forward trajectory. Q2'26 EBITDA of $71.0B annualizes to ~$284B, implying forward EV/EBITDA already well below 20x on current run-rate.

**Valuation verdict: Justified and arguably cheap on a forward/growth-adjusted basis.** TTM metrics optically elevated but rapidly compressing.

---

## 2. Profitability

### Revenue
TTM: **$253.49B**. Quarterly trajectory: $46.7B → $57.0B → $68.1B → $81.6B — accelerating sequential growth. Organic, driven by sustained AI infrastructure spending.

### Gross Margin: 74.1%
Exceptional for hardware. Reflects NVIDIA's pricing power and software-like economics. This margin has held and expanded as revenue scaled — disproves the margin compression thesis.

### Operating Margin: 65.6%
One of the highest of any large-cap industrial company globally. Achieved while scaling R&D ($6.3B in Q2'26 alone) in absolute dollars alongside revenue. Operating leverage is real.

### Net Profit Margin: 63.0%
$0.63 of every revenue dollar becomes net income. At $253.49B revenue scale, this is essentially unprecedented in industrial history. Typically associated with software monopolies or financial exchanges.

### ROE: 114.3% | ROA: 52.7%
ROE above 100% means net income exceeds total equity — exceptional capital efficiency. ROA above 50% reflects how little asset base is needed to generate earnings.

**Profitability verdict: Best-in-class across every metric. Trajectory (accelerating revenue, stable/expanding margins) is more important than the already exceptional levels.**

---

## 3. Balance Sheet

### Cash and Liquidity
- Cash & ST investments: **$80.57B** (Q2'26), up from $54.0B in Q3'25 (+$26.6B in three quarters from operating CF alone).
- Total debt: **$12.35B** → Net cash: approximately **$68B**.
- Fortress balance sheet. Company is generating cash faster than it can deploy through buybacks, capex, or acquisitions.

### D/E Ratio: 6.55 (data anomaly)
With equity of $195.5B and debt of $12.35B, actual D/E ≈ 0.063. Reported 6.55 is likely a data feed artifact or calculated on a different equity base. Balance sheet is effectively unlevered.

### Current Ratio: 3.44
Working capital grew $29B in one year (from $78.0B to $107.1B). No liquidity risk.

**Balance sheet verdict: Exceptional. Net cash ~$68B, current ratio 3.44, growing WC. Effectively debt-free operationally.**

---

## 4. Cash Flow

### Operating Cash Flow — Quarterly Ramp
$15.4B (Q3'25) → $23.8B (Q4'25) → $36.2B (Q1'26) → $50.3B (Q2'26). Annualizing Q2'26 = ~$201B operating CF run-rate.

### Capex: <2.5% of Revenue
$1.76B in Q2'26. Fabless model: TSMC bears fab investment. Drives extraordinary FCF conversion.

### Free Cash Flow
TTM FCF: **$46.34B** — understates current run-rate. Q2'26 alone generated **$48.59B** in FCF. Quarterly progression: $13.5B → $22.1B → $34.9B → $48.6B — one of the steepest FCF ramps in public market history.

### Capital Return
Stock buybacks: $9.7B + $12.5B + $3.8B + $19.3B = ~$45.3B over four quarters. Returning cash aggressively while growing the cash balance simultaneously — only possible when FCF overwhelms capital return programs.

**Cash flow verdict: Best-in-class and accelerating. FCF run-rate multiplied several times in four quarters.**

---

## 5. Growth

TTM EPS: $6.54 → Forward EPS: $12.68 → **93.9% implied EPS growth**.

Credibility check: Last 12 months of actual EPS: 121% growth from Q3'25 to Q2'26 ($1.08 to $2.39). Forward consensus of $12.68 requires ~$3.00–$3.50/quarter — plausible given Q2'26 produced $2.39 and trajectory is still accelerating.

Risk: not whether NVIDIA can grow, but whether the AI infrastructure spending cycle sustains. Hyperscaler capex commitments provide multi-year demand visibility.

**Growth verdict: Forward EPS estimate aggressive but grounded in demonstrable trajectory. Most recent quarterly run-rate is consistent.**

---

## 6. Analyst Consensus

- **Mean target: $298.07** — ~45–50% upside from implied current price (~$203/share)
- **High target: $500** — Bulls embedding sustained multi-year AI capex cycle
- **Low target: $180** — Bears embedding spending cycle deceleration
- **Recommendation: STRONG_BUY**

$320 spread between high and low reflects disagreement about AI capex sustainability, not about business quality.

---

## Overall Assessment

Current valuation is **justified given the demonstrated growth trajectory**, with the caveat that it requires the AI infrastructure spending cycle to remain intact for the next 12–18 months to validate forward estimates.

Primary risk: cycle concentration. 80%+ of revenue is AI/data center GPU demand from a small number of hyperscaler customers. If spending decelerates to 20–30% growth from current 70–90%, the Forward P/E would re-rate toward 25–30x on lower-than-expected earnings, creating meaningful downside.
