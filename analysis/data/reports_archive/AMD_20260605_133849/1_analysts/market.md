## Detailed Trend Analysis

### 1. Price Action — A Relentless Rally Into a One-Day Pullback

AMD has staged one of the sharpest moves in its recent history over the last four weeks. The stock opened May 7 at $417.07 and closed June 3 at $542.52 — a **29.9% gain in 20 trading days**. This is not a slow grind; it is a momentum-driven vertical move with minimal consolidation.

The move broke into stages:
- **Phase 1 (May 7–May 11):** Explosive gap from $408.46 to $458.79 (+12.3%) on heavy volume — May 8 saw 58.1M shares, the highest volume day in the window. This gap-up is the foundation of the entire rally and suggests institutional accumulation triggered by a catalyst (likely AI data center narrative reinforcement).
- **Phase 2 (May 12–May 19):** Consolidation/digestion between $414–$450. Volume contracted to 26–39M range. The stock held above May 7 levels — a bullish consolidation pattern. Bears had every opportunity to reverse this during seven sessions of sideways action and failed.
- **Phase 3 (May 20–June 3):** A second leg higher, even more powerful. AMD ran from $414.05 on May 19 to $542.52 on June 3 — **+31% in 11 trading days**. Volume was moderate (24–38M), meaning this was not panic-buying but steady institutional accumulation under momentum. The May 26 session at 38.5M with a close at $503.89 was the first close above $500 — psychologically significant.
- **June 4 (latest session):** A pullback day — close $523.20 from $542.52 high, down $19.32 (3.56%). Intraday low was $499.87. The stock tested and held the $500 psychological level before recovering. Context: sector-wide selloff driven by Broadcom's AI guidance disappointment, not AMD-specific. Volume was 29M — below the 58M spike day, suggesting controlled selling, not distribution panic.

**Current Price vs. Structure:** At $523.20, AMD is:
- **3.9% above EMA 10 ($503.63)** — healthy; not excessively extended short-term
- **47.9% above SMA 50 ($353.80)** — significantly extended over medium-term trend; this is typical in parabolic momentum phases but flags mean-reversion risk
- **114.0% above SMA 200 ($244.47)** — long-term secular bull trend dramatically confirmed; the 200-SMA is far below and rising
- **11.8% above VWMA 20 ($467.93)** — price is above the volume-weighted average of recent trading, suggesting the smart-money tape is supportive but stretched

### 3. SMA 50 ($353.80) and SMA 200 ($244.47) — Long-Term Trend Context

The SMA 50 at $353.80 and SMA 200 at $244.47 confirm a powerful long-term bull trend. The golden cross (50 above 200) has been in place for an extended period. The large spread between these averages and current price ($523.20) tells a story of either:

The correct interpretation depends on whether forward earnings estimates ($13.01 EPS) materialize. If AMD delivers $13 in forward EPS, the 50-SMA will "catch up" as the stock consolidates. If estimates disappoint, the 50-SMA at $353.80 becomes a plausible downside magnet — nearly 32% lower.

The MACD reading is nuanced and important:

- MACD value: **49.47**
- Signal line: **48.63**
- Histogram: **+0.84** — barely positive

The histogram at +0.84 after a 30% rally is a warning sign. A MACD histogram of +0.84 on a $523 stock means the momentum engine is nearly on empty. During the Phase 3 run (May 20–June 3), the histogram would have been expanding rapidly. Now it has compressed to near-zero.

This is not a sell signal by itself — the MACD line is still above the signal line, so the trend is technically bullish. But the compression signals that the current leg is losing energy. A cross of MACD below its signal line (which would turn the histogram negative) would be a short-term momentum sell signal. Given the current values are so close, this could happen within 1–2 sessions on continued weakness.

**Key watch:** If AMD rallies back through $530 on volume, MACD expands positively — momentum resumes. If it drifts below $510 for 2–3 days, MACD crosses bearish — caution flag.

### 7. ATR (14) = $29.38 — High Volatility, Size Down

AMD's Average True Range is **$29.38** — meaning the stock routinely moves nearly $30 per day. This is extreme volatility for an absolute dollar amount and has direct implications:

- **Stop loss minimum:** 1.5 × ATR = $44.07. Any stop within $44 of entry will likely be hit by normal daily noise.
- **Position sizing:** With Beta at 2.49, AMD moves 2.49x the S&P 500. A 5% S&P down day would translate to ~12.5% AMD decline. Position sizing must reflect this.
- **Entry zone width:** Given $29 daily ATR, a $5-wide entry range is irrelevant. Entry should be defined in $10–$15 zones.

The ATR expanding from approximately $15–$18 range earlier in the year to $29.38 now reflects the parabolic phase — heightened risk in both directions.
