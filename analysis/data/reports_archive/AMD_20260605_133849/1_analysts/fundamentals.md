## Overview

AMD presents a fascinating valuation puzzle: the trailing numbers look extreme, but the forward story is one of the most compelling earnings inflection narratives in the semiconductor sector. At $523.20/share and a market cap of $810.46 billion, AMD is priced as if the forward EPS of $13.01 is virtually certain. The question is whether that certainty is justified.

---

## Valuation Analysis

### Trailing vs. Forward Divergence

The gap between AMD's TTM and forward metrics is extraordinary:

| Metric | TTM Value | Forward/Implication |
|--------|----------|---------------------|
| EPS | $2.98 | $13.01 (forward) |
| P/E | 166.79x | 38.21x (forward) |
| EV/EBITDA | 113.68x | Likely 25–30x forward |
| PEG Ratio | 1.31 | Reasonable if growth materializes |

**TTM P/E of 166.79x** is objectively expensive on a trailing basis. If AMD were a mature, slow-growth company, this valuation would be indefensible. But it isn't.

**Forward P/E of 38.21x** is the number that actually matters for forward-looking investors. At $523 with $13.01 expected EPS, you're paying 38x next year's earnings — which for a semiconductor company with AMD's growth trajectory is aggressive-but-defensible, not unreasonable. For context, NVIDIA traded at similar or higher forward multiples during its data center ramp.

**PEG of 1.31** is the most favorable valuation metric. PEG = Forward P/E ÷ Expected EPS Growth Rate. If AMD's EPS grows from $2.98 to $13.01, that's approximately 336% growth. A PEG of 1.31 on 300%+ growth actually implies the stock is *cheap* relative to growth — though PEG ratios at this level of growth must be used cautiously (growth rate denominator inflates the ratio favorably).

**Key question on valuation:** The forward P/E of 38x compresses to *very* attractive multiples if AMD executes on $13.01 EPS and continues growing in FY2027+. The stock is priced for execution, not for failure.

## Profitability Analysis

### Revenue Trajectory

| Quarter | Revenue | QoQ Change |
|---------|---------|-----------|
| Q2'25 | $7.685B | — |
| Q3'25 | $9.246B | +20.3% |
| Q4'25 | $10.270B | +11.1% |
| Q1'26 | $10.253B | -0.2% (flat) |

AMD grew revenue from $7.685B in Q2'25 to $10.270B in Q4'25 — a 33.6% increase in two quarters. The Q1'26 plateau at $10.253B is a yellow flag that deserves monitoring, but it is not alarming for several reasons:
- Q4 is traditionally a seasonally strong quarter for data center (hyperscaler year-end budgets)
- Q1 historically digests Q4 exuberance before the next ramp
- Revenue being flat QoQ after three consecutive strong growth quarters is more consolidation than deterioration

**TTM Revenue: $37.45 billion.** This represents AMD's full-year annualized run rate.

### Margin Analysis

| Metric | Q1'26 | Q4'25 | Q3'25 | Q2'25 |
|--------|-------|-------|-------|-------|
| Gross Profit | $5.416B | $5.577B | $4.780B | $3.059B |

**Gross margin** expanded dramatically from 39.8% (Q2'25) to 54.3% (Q4'25) — a 14.5 percentage point improvement in two quarters. This reflects mix shift toward high-margin data center AI products (MI300X carries higher margins than consumer or embedded chips). Q1'26 gross margin at 52.8% is a slight dip from Q4'25's peak but remains structurally elevated.

**Operating margin at 14.4% (TTM)** is modest for a company with 53% gross margins, reflecting the massive R&D and SG&A investment required to compete with NVIDIA. R&D spend is $2.397B in Q1'26 alone — AMD is investing heavily to narrow the software/ecosystem gap with CUDA. This is the right long-term decision but compresses near-term margins.

**ROE of 8.1% and ROA of 3.6%** appear low relative to the stock price, but both are artificially suppressed by:
1. Massive goodwill ($41.5B from Xilinx acquisition) inflating the equity base
2. The earnings inflection only recently materializing — ROE/ROA will improve significantly as forward EPS materializes

## Balance Sheet Analysis

### Cash and Debt

| Item | Q1'26 | Q4'25 | Q3'25 | Q2'25 |
|------|-------|-------|-------|-------|
| Cash & ST Invest | $12.347B | $10.552B | $7.243B | $5.867B |
| Total Debt | $3.871B | $3.847B | $3.870B | $3.886B |
| Net Cash Position | +$8.476B | +$6.705B | +$3.373B | +$1.981B |

**AMD has been rapidly accumulating cash.** Net cash position grew from $1.98B in Q2'25 to $8.48B in Q1'26 — more than quadrupled in four quarters. This is a direct consequence of FCF acceleration (see below). A net cash position of $8.5B on a $810B market cap is modest (1.05% of market cap) but growing rapidly.

**Debt is stable and low:** $3.87B with D/E ratio of 6.00 — this ratio looks alarming but is calculated against equity base inflated by Xilinx intangibles. Tangible book value equity is $22.96B, making the effective D/E closer to 0.17 — virtually no leverage on a tangible basis. AMD is not a leveraged company.

**Current ratio of 2.73** indicates strong short-term liquidity — AMD can comfortably cover near-term obligations.

### Goodwill and Intangibles Risk

Total goodwill + intangibles: **$41.498B** — representing 52% of total assets ($79.642B). This is a legacy of the Xilinx acquisition and a genuine risk: if AMD's embedded/adaptive computing business underperforms, goodwill impairment charges could be material. However, there is no current indication of impairment.

Tangible book value per share: $22.964B / ~1,600M shares ≈ **$14.35/share** — the stock trades at ~36x tangible book. This is purely a bet on AMD's AI and data center earnings power, not on asset value.

### Inventory Build

|---------|-----------|
| Q2'25 | $6.677B |
| Q3'25 | $7.313B |
| Q4'25 | $7.920B |

Inventory has grown from $6.677B to $8.045B — a 20.5% increase over four quarters. Two interpretations:
1. **Bullish:** AMD is pre-positioning inventory for anticipated demand surge (MI300X production ramp); strategic build-ahead

Given the revenue growth has been strong (Q2→Q4'25), the inventory build is more likely preparation for continued demand than excess stock. But if Q2'26 revenue disappoints, the inventory line becomes a red flag.

## Cash Flow Analysis

### Free Cash Flow — The Strongest Indicator

| Quarter | Operating CF | Capex | Free CF |
|---------|-------------|-------|---------|
| Q2'25 | $2.011B | -$282M | $1.729B |
| Q3'25 | $2.159B | -$258M | $1.901B |
| Q4'25 | $2.600B | -$222M | $2.378B |
| Q1'26 | $2.955B | -$389M | $2.566B |

**This is AMD's most compelling fundamental story.** FCF has grown from $1.729B to $2.566B per quarter in four quarters — a 48% increase. Annualized Q1'26 FCF run rate is approximately **$10.26B**. At $810B market cap, AMD trades at roughly **79x annualized FCF** — expensive but declining rapidly as FCF accelerates.

**Capex is intentionally low:** AMD is a fabless semiconductor company. It designs chips but outsources manufacturing to TSMC. This means AMD's FCF conversion (FCF/Net Income) is high — in Q1'26, FCF was $2.566B vs. net income $1.383B. FCF exceeded net income because AMD front-loaded stock compensation (non-cash) and had positive working capital changes.

**Stock buybacks:** AMD repurchased $355M of stock in Q1'26 — modest relative to FCF of $2.566B. AMD is prioritizing cash retention and R&D over buybacks, which is the correct strategic choice during an AI arms race.

## Growth Analysis

### EPS Inflection — The Core of the Bull Case

| Quarter | EPS (Diluted) |
|---------|--------------|
| Q2'25 | $0.54 |
| Q3'25 | $0.75 |
| Q4'25 | $0.92 |
| Q1'26 | $0.84 |
| TTM | $2.98 |
| **Forward (Annual)** | **$13.01** |

The EPS trajectory shows strong sequential growth through Q4'25, followed by a slight dip in Q1'26 ($0.92 → $0.84). The forward annual EPS estimate of $13.01 implies annualized quarterly EPS of approximately $3.25/quarter — roughly 4x Q1'26 levels. This is a massive implied acceleration.

**What drives $13.01 forward EPS?**
- AI GPU revenue: MI300X becoming a meaningful revenue contributor as hyperscalers qualify and ramp
- EPYC server CPU: continued share gains from Intel in data centers
- Software monetization: ROCm ecosystem gaining enterprise adoption
- Operating leverage: R&D spend as a % of revenue declining as revenue scales

This estimate requires AMD to roughly double-to-triple its quarterly run rate from $10.25B (Q1'26) to approximately $15–17B/quarter by Q3–Q4'26. Ambitious but not impossible — NVIDIA achieved similar acceleration during its data center ramp.

**How to interpret insider selling:**
- **10b5-1 plans:** Most executive selling is conducted under pre-scheduled Rule 10b5-1 plans filed months in advance, which reduces the information content of any single transaction. If Lisa Su's May 13 sale was pursuant to a plan filed in January 2026, it is less alarming.
- **Scale:** $55.69M is meaningful even for an executive. AMD's total insider selling over the past 3 months has been concentrated and heavy.
- **Timing:** The sales occurred during the rally, not during a correction. If insiders were truly concerned about near-term headwinds, they would accelerate selling — which appears to be happening.

**Assessment:** The insider selling is a **yellow flag, not a red flag**. It does not necessarily signal the executives believe the stock is at a peak, but it does confirm they are taking chips off the table at current prices. Investors should note this as a risk factor but not treat it as a definitive sell signal.

## Fundamentals Analyst Verdict

AMD's fundamental picture is a tale of two timeframes:

**Short term (TTM):** Expensive by every conventional metric. P/E 167x, EV/EBITDA 114x, stock above analyst mean target. Insiders selling. Q1'26 revenue slightly declined QoQ. Operating margin 14.4% is modest.

**Long term (Forward):** Compelling. FCF accelerating to $10B+ annualized run rate. Cash fortress growing rapidly ($12.35B, net cash $8.5B). Forward P/E 38x for a company targeting $13.01 EPS growth is defensible. PEG 1.31 is reasonable. AI data center ramp is real and confirmed by cash flow generation.

**Key risk:** The $13.01 forward EPS estimate is heavily dependent on AMD's AI data center revenue ramping dramatically in calendar 2026. If the AI capex cycle slows (Broadcom's guidance is a warning flag), this estimate could be revised downward materially, resetting the forward P/E upward and triggering significant stock price decline.

**Key opportunity:** If AMD delivers $13+ EPS in FY2026, the stock at 38x forward earnings is a bargain for a company with this FCF profile. The bull case is that $523 today looks cheap by year-end 2026.
