## Debate Evaluation

### Which Side Won?

**The Bull won the long-term structural argument. The Bear won the near-term risk/reward argument.** Both cannot be dismissed — this is a genuine two-timeframe split.

**Why the Bull's strongest arguments hold up:**
1. **FCF is real and accelerating.** $2.566B in Q1'26 FCF is not spin. $12.35B in cash growing from $5.87B in four quarters is not spin. The bear's SBC adjustment to $2.079B "owner earnings" is valid but doesn't change the direction: AMD is a powerful cash-generating machine with the flywheel spinning.
2. **Forward P/E of 38.21x is defensible for this growth rate.** If AMD delivers anything close to $13.01 EPS, the current price looks cheap in retrospect. PEG of 1.31 — while imperfect as a metric for step-change growth — signals the multiple is not disconnected from the growth story.
3. **Revenue trajectory is strong on a YoY basis.** The QoQ plateau is a legitimate concern, but the YoY comparison from Q1'25 to Q1'26 is almost certainly 30%+. Bulls are correct that Q1 seasonality is a factor.
4. **AMD's AI narrative is distinct from Broadcom.** The bear conflates Broadcom's ASIC-specific guidance with AMD's GPU/CPU product line. Different customers, different deployment timelines, different revenue drivers.

**Why the Bear's strongest arguments cannot be dismissed:**
1. **$523 is $40 above analyst mean consensus.** This is a hard fact that limits near-term upside. Stocks that trade above consensus require continuous positive estimate revisions to sustain the premium. One earnings miss reprices them below consensus fast.
2. **Insider selling of $77M+ is a material flag.** The bull's "10b5-1 plan" argument is valid but incomplete — the scale ($77M) and concentration at all-time highs is still a yellow flag that sophisticated investors track.
3. **Revenue deceleration is real.** Q4'25 to Q1'26 went from $10.270B to $10.253B. This is not catastrophic, but for a stock priced for acceleration, deceleration at the margin creates compression risk.
4. **The risk/reward asymmetry is real at $523.** Bull's 27% upside to $665 vs. Bear's 35% downside on an estimate miss to ~$342. Entry price matters enormously for this stock.

### What the Bear Got Wrong
- The bear treated MACD and other short-term momentum indicators as if they invalidate the fundamental case. They don't. MACD is a lagging indicator that reflects recent price changes, not business trajectory.
- The bear's "wait for $460–$480" argument is disciplined but potentially leaves money on the table if Q2'26 earnings beat and the stock gaps to $600 without revisiting those levels.
- Conflating Broadcom's ASIC business guidance with AMD's GPU/CPU demand was intellectually imprecise.

### What the Bull Got Wrong
- The bull waved away the revenue plateau too quickly. "Seasonal" is a convenient explanation but Q1'26 revenue flatness deserves scrutiny, especially since AI data center deployments are supposed to be ramping.
- "Consensus will catch up" is a faith-based argument. It requires AMD to execute perfectly at a time when sector-wide AI capex questions are being raised by Broadcom.
- The bull understated the risk asymmetry at current levels. Even in the best case, buying at $523 vs $499–503 leaves unnecessary risk on the table.
