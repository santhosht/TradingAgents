## Opening Argument — Protect Capital First

My colleagues are excited about the AMD opportunity. I want to be the voice that asks: what does this trade look like if we're wrong?

**Let me start with the price reality:** AMD closed at $523.20. The analyst mean price target is $482.69. We are proposing to buy AMD at $503 — which is still $20 above analyst consensus. We are proposing to buy a stock that the average professional who models this company for a living says is *already above fair value*. That is not the definition of a margin of safety.

### The Insider Selling Must Be Taken Seriously

The aggressive analyst dismisses insider selling as "portfolio management." Let me quantify it differently:

- Lisa Su sold $55.69M on May 13 — when AMD was near $445/share based on the timing
- Mark Papermaster sold over $21M in multiple tranches across April–May
- This is not one sale by one executive — it is a *pattern* of selling across multiple officers

When you combine: (a) price above analyst consensus, (b) multiple insiders selling millions during the rally, and (c) revenue growth decelerating — you have three concurrent yellow flags. Each alone is explainable. All three together should give any conservative investor pause.

### The Downside Scenario Is Not Hypothetical

Let me construct the bear scenario with numbers:
- AMD misses Q2'26 revenue consensus (say, $9.8B vs expected $11B+)
- Forward EPS guidance revised from $13.01 to $9.50
- Stock reprices at 38x new forward EPS = 38 × $9.50 = **$361**
- That is a 28% decline from the $503 entry — **a loss of $142 per share**, or $14,200 per 100 shares

At a 4% portfolio allocation on a $500,000 portfolio, that's $20,000 in AMD → if stopped out at $459, loss = ($503-$459)/$503 = 8.7% × $20,000 = **$1,742**. That is manageable.

But here's my concern: the $459 stop does NOT protect against a gap-down. If AMD reports Q2 earnings below expectations and the stock opens at $430 Monday morning, you do not exit at $459 — you exit at $430. Actual loss: ($503-$430)/$503 = 14.5% × $20,000 = **$2,900**. The stop provides false precision against gap risk.

**This is the real risk of holding through an earnings event.**

### My Concerns About the Proposed Trade

**1. Entry is still above consensus ($482):**
Even at $503, we are entering above the analyst mean target. For a conservative approach, entering *below* consensus provides a true margin of safety. The $467–$475 zone (Bollinger midline, VWMA-20) is where consensus and technical support both converge. That is where I would establish the core position, not $503.

If that means we miss the trade because AMD never pulls back to $467 — so be it. There are always other opportunities. What I cannot accept is buying above analyst consensus and hoping the estimates get revised higher on our timeline.

**2. The revenue deceleration is not dismissed by "seasonality":**
Q4'25 to Q1'26 revenue: -$17M. This is the *data center ramp quarter* — the period when AMD's MI300X deployments should be accelerating, not plateauing. If the data center ramp is pushing quarterly revenue from $10.270B to $10.253B (essentially flat), when does the $16–17B/quarter needed for $13 EPS happen? The timeline required for $13.01 forward EPS is Q3–Q4 2026. That gives AMD two more quarters to double its run rate. The Q1 flat is not encouraging.

**3. Earnings risk is the dominant near-term event:**
AMD's next earnings report is approximately 8–10 weeks away. Between now and then, there is no fundamental catalyst other than the AI narrative — and Broadcom's guidance has raised questions about AI capex timing. The most disciplined approach: size the position conservatively now (2%), and evaluate whether to add *after* the earnings call when the $13 EPS trajectory is either confirmed or challenged.

**4. Position sizing should be 3% maximum:**
The aggressive analyst advocates 4%. I advocate 3%, maximum. Here is why: AMD has Beta 2.49. In a 10% S&P market correction, AMD could fall 25%. On a 3% portfolio allocation, that's a 0.75% portfolio loss — uncomfortable but manageable. On a 4% allocation, it's 1.0% — approaching a scenario where a bad market day materially impacts your total portfolio.

### Rebuttal to Aggressive Analyst

The aggressive analyst says: "The risk of not getting in is as real as the risk of losing money." This is a classic momentum trader's rationalization for abandoning discipline. The risk of being *not invested* is not comparable to the risk of active capital loss. Cash earns the risk-free rate and preserves optionality. AMD at $503 today might be AMD at $467 in two weeks — patience is a position.

The aggressive analyst also says not to stage the position because "buying at $467 means buying below EMA-10 which is a breakpoint." But that is exactly wrong — the Bollinger midline at $471 and VWMA at $467 are *natural support levels*, not breakpoints. A stock pulling back to its 20-day moving average after a 33% run is not in a "broken" trend — it is in a healthy correction.

### Rebuttal to Neutral Analyst

The neutral analyst will propose compromise between my conservatism and the aggressive view. I accept the 4% target allocation — I simply stage it differently. The destination (4% in AMD) may be the same; the timing of entries (which preserves capital if the thesis is wrong in the short term) is where I diverge.

**Summary:** AMD is a legitimate long-term holding. But the current price offers insufficient margin of safety for a large immediate position. Enter 2% at $499–$507, protect capital above all else, and add only when the AI data center narrative is confirmed by actual Q2 earnings data.
