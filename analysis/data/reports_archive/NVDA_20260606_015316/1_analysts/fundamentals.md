# NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) — Fundamental Analysis Report
**As of June 6, 2026 | Market Cap: $4,967.73B**

---

## Executive Summary

NVIDIA has transformed from a graphics chip company into the foundational infrastructure layer of the AI economy. The core analytical question is not whether NVIDIA is expensive in absolute terms, but whether its growth trajectory justifies its current multiples. On a forward and PEG-adjusted basis, NVIDIA is arguably **undervalued relative to its growth rate** — a rare combination at nearly $5 trillion in market capitalization.

---

## 1. Valuation Analysis

### Headline Multiples

At first glance, a P/E of 31.46x (TTM) and a Price/Book of 31.69x appear elevated. But these trailing figures are deeply misleading given the velocity of NVIDIA's earnings growth. The forward P/E of **16.17x** — based on consensus forward EPS of $12.68 versus TTM EPS of $6.52 — implies the market is pricing in near-doubling of earnings within the next twelve months. That is not speculation; it is extrapolation from an already-demonstrated quarterly cadence.

The **PEG ratio of 0.66** is the single most important valuation anchor in this report. A PEG below 1.0 conventionally signals undervaluation relative to growth. For a company compounding earnings at NVIDIA's rate while carrying minimal debt, 0.66x is a significant discount to intrinsic value.

**EV/EBITDA of 29.75x** is similarly less alarming when contextualized: NVIDIA generated $165.51B in EBITDA on a TTM basis, growing at an annualized rate exceeding 100% year-over-year.

### Price-to-FCF

TTM FCF is $46.34B against a market cap of $4,967.73B, implying a Price/FCF of approximately **107x on a trailing basis**. However, Q2'26 alone produced $48.587B in FCF — annualizing the most recent quarter yields a run-rate FCF of ~$194B, compressing the forward Price/FCF to approximately **25.6x**.

### Analyst Consensus

The analyst mean target of $298.07 against the current implied price of approximately **$204.40** per share suggests **~46% upside to the mean target**. The high target of $500 implies 145% upside. The bear case at $180 is only marginally below current levels. The consensus recommendation is **STRONG BUY**.

**Valuation Verdict: Stretched on trailing absolute metrics, but justified-to-cheap on forward and growth-adjusted metrics. PEG of 0.66 at this scale is exceptional.**

## 2. Profitability Analysis

### Gross Margin: 74.1%

This is software-company territory in a hardware business. The progression from Q3'25 to Q2'26 — $33.853B, $41.849B, $51.093B, $61.157B gross profit on sequential revenue growth — demonstrates not just scale but expanding per-unit economics. Gross margin has remained consistently above 73% throughout this period, confirming pricing power is intact and input cost inflation is not eroding the spread.

### Operating Margin: 65.6%

An operating margin above 65% at $253B in revenue is extraordinary. For comparison, Apple operates at approximately 30% operating margins; Microsoft Azure at roughly 45%.

### Net Margin: 63.0%

Net income of $159.61B TTM on $253.49B in revenue. Every $1.00 of revenue generates $0.63 in net income after all taxes, interest, and operating costs. Platform monopoly economics.

### Return on Equity: 114.3% | Return on Assets: 52.7%

ROA of 52.7% means NVIDIA generates $0.527 in net income for every $1.00 of assets — confirms the asset-light nature of the business model.

**Profitability Verdict: Best-in-class across all major metrics. Gross and operating margins at levels typically reserved for pure software platforms.**

## 3. Balance Sheet Analysis

### Liquidity

Cash and short-term investments of $80.572B against total debt of $12.348B yields a **net cash position of $68.224B**. Current ratio of 3.44 confirms no near-term liquidity risk. Working capital of $107.111B growing rapidly.

### Debt Structure

Total debt of $12.348B is negligible against the earnings base. NVIDIA generated more net income in Q2'26 alone ($58.321B) than its entire debt load. The D/E ratio of 6.55 sounds alarming but reflects aggressive buybacks reducing the book equity denominator, not dangerous leverage.

### Asset Trajectory

Total assets grew from $140.740B (Q3'25) to $259.474B (Q2'26) — an 84% increase in three quarters. Primarily driven by cash accumulation, receivables growth, and inventory build. Inventory of $25.797B reflects strategic buffer stock during Blackwell ramp — rational during chronic demand-supply imbalance.

**Balance Sheet Verdict: Fortress. Net cash of $68B, negligible debt relative to earnings power. The balance sheet is a weapon, not a constraint.**

## 4. Cash Flow Analysis

### Operating Cash Flow Inflection

| Quarter | Operating CF |
|---|---|
| Q3'25 | $15.365B |
| Q4'25 | $23.751B |
| Q1'26 | $36.188B |
| Q2'26 | $50.344B |

This is not linear growth — it is acceleration. Sequential quarterly increases: $8.386B, $12.437B, $14.156B. Annualized run-rate exceeds $200B.

### Capital Expenditure: Remarkably Low

Capex of $1.757B in Q2'26 against $81.615B in revenue equals a capex intensity of **2.2%**. NVIDIA designs the chips; TSMC manufactures them; hyperscalers house them. NVIDIA captures the highest-margin layer while bearing minimal capital risk.

### Free Cash Flow

FCF of $48.587B in Q2'26 alone — annualizing to approximately $194B. Apple generates approximately $100B in annual FCF. NVIDIA is on track to exceed that within the current fiscal year.

### Capital Allocation

Buybacks of $19.312B in Q2'26 — acceleration from $3.815B in Q1'26. Total buybacks of $45.303B over last four quarters against FCF of ~$119B = ~38% payout ratio. Disciplined and return-focused.

**Cash Flow Verdict: FCF inflection is exceptional. Low capex intensity combined with hypergrowth revenue creates one of the most powerful FCF generation engines in corporate history.**

### Earnings Growth

EPS progression: $1.08 → $1.30 → $1.76 → $2.39 (Q3'25 through Q2'26). Q2'26 EPS was 121% higher than Q3'25 in just three quarters. Forward EPS of $12.68 implies ~94.5% forward growth.

**Growth Verdict: Revenue and earnings growth among the fastest ever recorded at this revenue scale. The growth rate is not decelerating.**

## 6. Key Risks

1. **Customer Concentration:** Hyperscalers (Microsoft, Google, Amazon, Meta) represent a large share of revenue. Coordinated capex pullback would be immediately impactful.
2. **TSMC Dependency:** Geopolitical risk around Taiwan represents a supply chain tail risk that cannot be fully hedged.
3. **Export Controls:** U.S. restrictions on chip exports to China have already limited a significant addressable market.
4. **Competitive Entry:** AMD, Intel, Google (TPU), Amazon (Trainium), and Microsoft (Maia) all investing in alternative AI silicon. The CUDA moat is durable but not permanent.
5. **Margin Sustainability:** 74%+ gross margins reflect current pricing power. Competitive alternatives maturing = margin compression risk.

---

## 7. Valuation Conclusion

**Current valuation is JUSTIFIED relative to growth, with selective metrics that appear CHEAP on a forward basis.**

The TTM P/E of 31.46x is irrelevant when EPS is doubling annually. The forward P/E of 16.17x is below the S&P 500 historical average (~18x) — the fastest-growing large company in the world trading at a forward multiple below the broad market average. The PEG of 0.66 signals undervaluation by conventional growth-adjusted frameworks.

At $204 with a mean analyst target of $298 and a PEG of 0.66, the risk/reward remains skewed to the upside on any 12-month horizon.
