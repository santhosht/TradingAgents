## Structural Problems in the Current Trading Plan

**1. Breakout entry stop almost untenable.** $213 entry with $199 stop = $14/share risk. On 1% budget ($1,000 / $100K), only 71 shares ($15,100 face value). Too small to matter. Either size up (breaks budget) or accept it is a lottery ticket.

**2. Stop level ($199) is identical across all tranches, creating wildly different R/R.** Tranche 1 from $205 = $6 risk. Tranche 2 from $200 = $1 risk. Breakout from $213 = $14 risk. If Tranche 2 is "higher conviction," position size should scale accordingly: $1,000 / $1/share = 1,000 shares from $200 entry (same dollar risk, 6x the shares).

**3. Raise-stop-to-breakeven at T1 creates a trap in chop scenario.** If stock bounces to $218 then chops $210–$220, a pullback to $205 stops you out of a still-valid trade. Use a time-anchored rule: raise stop to breakeven only after T1 holds for at least 3 trading days.

**4. No mechanism for "opened below $203" re-entry scenario.** If June 6 opens at $201, stabilizes, and closes at $204, Tranche 1 conditions technically fail (requires open above $203). But a close at $204 after touching $201 and bouncing is a stronger signal than a straight drift to $204. The open-price condition is too rigid.

**5. Hyperscaler capex exit trigger has no execution guidance.** "Exit regardless of stop level" is correct to include — but no size/timing/order type guidance means it functions as aspiration rather than instruction.

But a lower-highs structure since May 14, unresolved MACD deterioration, 35% probability of SMA 50 failure, and $218.60 overhead supply means conditions that justify confidence are not yet established. The correct response: 40% starter at $204–$207 conditional on SMA 50 holding at the close, 1.0–1.2% risk budget on that tranche, dry powder reserved for the $198–$202 deep-flush scenario, hard stop at $199 close-only, T1 at $218.60 as a partial-exit and stop-raise milestone (not a hold-through event). The aggressive analyst is wrong on position sizing and timing certainty; the conservative analyst is wrong on reserving most capital for a minority-probability scenario. The actual risk lies in failing to define exactly which conditions must be met before each tranche fires — the structural gap the current plan has not fully closed.
